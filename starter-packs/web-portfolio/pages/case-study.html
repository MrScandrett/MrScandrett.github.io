How one project changed after feedback

A case study is not a longer description. It is the story of a decision: what went wrong, what somebody told you, and what you did about it. Keep every section below to a short paragraph. Replace every word of the sample text with your own.

1. The problem

Replace this. State the problem in one or two sentences, before any solution is mentioned. Who was it for, and what had to be true for it to count as solved?

2. My first attempt

Replace this. Describe what you built first and why it seemed right at the time. Do not tidy up the history: a first attempt that was too complicated, or too fast, or unreadable, is the interesting part.

3. The feedback

Replace this. Say who tested it and what they actually did, not what they said politely. "Three people all clicked the wrong thing first" is feedback. "They liked it" is not.

“Replace this with a real sentence somebody said while using it.”

4. What I changed

Replace this. Name the specific change and the reason. One clear change explained properly beats a list of five vague ones. If you decided not to change something, say that too, and say why.

5. The result

Replace this. What is measurably different now? Then finish with the honest part: what is still unfinished, and what you would do next if you had another week.
