The recursive idea

To move a tower of n disks from peg A to peg C:

  1. Move the top n − 1 disks from A to B (using C as the spare).
  2. Move the biggest disk from A to C.
  3. Move the n − 1 disks from B onto C (using A as the spare).

Steps 1 and 3 are the same puzzle with one fewer disk. Each extra disk doubles the work, plus one move, so n disks need 2n − 1 moves.

About the puzzle

The French mathematician Édouard Lucas invented the puzzle in 1883 and sold it as a toy. It came with a made-up legend: in a temple, priests move 64 golden disks between three diamond needles, and when they finish, the world will end.

At one move per second with no mistakes, 64 disks take 264 − 1 = 18,446,744,073,709,551,615 moves: about 585 billion years, over 40 times the age of the universe.

The puzzle is a favorite first example of recursion in computer science: a problem solved by solving smaller copies of itself.
