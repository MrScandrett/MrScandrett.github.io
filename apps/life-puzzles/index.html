The rules

  • A living cell with 2 or 3 living neighbors stays alive.
  • An empty cell with exactly 3 living neighbors comes alive.
  • Every other cell is empty next generation (too lonely or too crowded).

Neighbors are the 8 cells around a cell, diagonals included.

About the puzzle

The mathematician John Conway invented the Game of Life in 1970, and Martin Gardner made it famous in his Scientific American column that year.

From three simple rules come shapes that sit still, shapes that pulse, and gliders that crawl across the grid. Life can even simulate a complete computer, which is why it is a favorite example of complex behavior from simple rules.
