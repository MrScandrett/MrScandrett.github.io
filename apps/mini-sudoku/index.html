How the computer solves it

It finds the empty square with the fewest possible numbers, tries one, and moves on. If it gets stuck, it backtracks and tries the next number: the same idea as the Eight Queens exhibit.

About the puzzle

Modern Sudoku was invented by Howard Garns, a retired architect from Indiana, and published in 1979 as "Number Place". It became a craze in Japan in the 1980s, where it got the name Sudoku ("single number"), and went worldwide in 2005.

There are only 288 ways to fill a 4 × 4 grid correctly, but about 6.7 sextillion (6,670,903,752,021,072,936,960) ways to fill a 9 × 9 one. A good puzzle has exactly one answer: every puzzle here is checked by the computer to make sure it does.
