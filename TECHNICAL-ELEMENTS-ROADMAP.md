# Technical Elements Curriculum Roadmap

Last updated: 2026-08-01

## Course Goal

Technical Elements is a grades 7–12 school-to-professional A/V production pathway. Students should leave able to explain the science, operate equipment safely, communicate within a production team, follow a signal path, and diagnose failures with evidence.

Existing physics, mathematics, engineering, and design lessons remain canonical in their original modules and are cross-listed here when they directly support production work.

## Phase 0 — Sound Science Foundations

Status: live through existing lessons.

- Point Wave
- Physics of Music
- Fourier Series & Transform
- Cymatics: The Geometry of Sound
- Digital Signal Processing

Outcome: students can describe frequency, amplitude, propagation, harmonics, interference, resonance, standing waves, sampling, and filters.

## Phase 1 — Core Audio Engineering

Status: live.

- Live Audio Signal Chain
- Microphone Polar Pattern Lab
- Room Acoustics & Reverb Lab
- EQ: Find the Problem Frequency

Outcome: students can trace and troubleshoot a basic sound system, select and place microphones, manage gain before feedback, estimate the effect of a room, and make restrained corrective-EQ decisions.

## Phase 2 — Lighting, Cameras & Video

Status: Stage Lighting and Optics are live; five production labs are planned.

1. DMX Lighting & Addressing
2. Camera Exposure Triangle
3. White Balance & Color Temperature
4. Video Signal Chain
5. Projection & Throw-Ratio Lab

Outcome: students can patch and address lighting, expose and color-match cameras, route production video, and engineer projection for a venue.

## Phase 3 — Live Production Systems & Capstone

Status: planned.

1. Livestream Control Room Simulator
2. Wireless Microphone & RF Coordination
3. Rigging, Loads & Safety Factors
4. Production Troubleshooting Escape Room

Outcome: students can work from a rundown, communicate cues, plan safe and redundant systems, coordinate wireless equipment, isolate faults, and run a supervised school production.

## Lesson Completion Contract

Each new Technical Elements lesson should include:

- A real production problem or design brief
- The underlying science, technology, engineering, art, and mathematics
- An interactive simulator or measurable hands-on activity
- School-level equipment examples with a bridge to professional practice
- Safety boundaries and adult-supervision requirements where relevant
- A troubleshooting scenario
- A student deliverable or production log
- A short knowledge check or exit ticket
- Previous/next links within the pathway

## Recommended Build Order

1. DMX Lighting & Addressing
2. Camera Exposure Triangle
3. Video Signal Chain
4. White Balance & Color Temperature
5. Livestream Control Room Simulator
6. Projection & Throw-Ratio Lab
7. Wireless Microphone & RF Coordination
8. Rigging, Loads & Safety Factors
9. Production Troubleshooting Escape Room

The troubleshooting capstone comes last because it should reuse failure modes and vocabulary introduced by every earlier lab.
