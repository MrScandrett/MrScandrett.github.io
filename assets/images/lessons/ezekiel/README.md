# Ezekiel artwork & archaeological artifacts

The lesson uses three historical, public-domain artworks reviewed for classroom suitability. Each opens at full size in the lightbox. These are later visual interpretations of the passages, not evidence of Ezekiel's physical appearance or events.

- `dry-bones-manuscript.jpg`: Illustration of a robed Ezekiel and a valley of bones, reproduced as fig. 5 in J. Henry Middleton's *Illuminated Manuscripts in Classical and Mediaeval Times* (1892), after a ninth-century Byzantine manuscript image. [Wikimedia Commons file and public-domain record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Illuminated_Manuscripts_(Middleton)_figure05.jpg).
- `rosselli-prophet-ezekiel.jpg`: Francesco Rosselli, *The Prophet Ezekiel*, from *Prophets and Sibyls*, 1480–90. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 29.16.19, Open Access / public domain. [Museum object record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/625971).
- `merian-chariot-vision.jpg`: Matthäus Merian, illustration of Ezekiel 1 for *Icones Biblicae*, before 1650. [Wikimedia Commons file and public-domain record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ezekiel-Vision-Merkaba.jpg).

The two archaeological photographs below were retrieved 2026-10-10. JPEGs preserve high-resolution reproductions; WebP previews are resized derivatives without cropping. Full JPEGs open in the lesson lightbox (`data-full`).
- `jehoiachin-ration-tablet.jpg`: *Jehoiachin's Rations Tablet (Babylon 28186)*, c. 592 BCE, Neo-Babylonian cuneiform clay administrative tablet from the Southern Palace at Babylon; Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin. Documents sesame oil and barley allocations issued by Nebuchadnezzar II's administration to *"Ya'u-kinu, king of the land of Yahuda"* (King Jehoiachin of Judah) and five Judean royal princes living in Babylonian captivity. Corroborates the exact dating anchor of Ezekiel 1:2: *"the fifth year of the exile of King Jehoiachin"*. Public domain. [Wikimedia Commons file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jehoiachin_Ration_Tablet.JPG).
- `ishtar-gate-dragon.jpg`: *Ishtar Gate Mušḫuššu (Dragon of Marduk)*, c. 575 BCE, molded and glazed polychrome brick relief from the gateway of Babylon; Vorderasiatisches Museum / Pergamon Museum, Berlin (VAG 00445). Depicts the composite creature—head and tail of a serpent, front paws of a lion, rear talons of an eagle—sacred to the patron god of Babylon. Illustrates the pagan imperial religious iconography and monumental architecture that surrounded Ezekiel and the exiles along the Chebar canal (Ezekiel 1:1–3, 8:7–12). Public domain. [Wikimedia Commons file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ishtar_Gate_Dragon.JPG).

The artworks distinguish biblical testimony from later artistic visualization; the photographs show physical artifacts from the Neo-Babylonian world.

Four more artworks were added 2026-10-29 from The Metropolitan Museum of Art's Open Access collection (public domain, retrieved through the Met collection API). JPEGs are resized to at most 2000 px; WebP previews are resized to at most 1100 px, uncropped.
- `ghisi-sistine-ezekiel.jpg`: Giorgio Ghisi after Michelangelo, *The Prophet Ezekiel*, from *Prophets and Sibyls in the Sistine Chapel*, 1570–75, engraving. The Met, 1984.1108.2. [Museum object record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/360376).
- `pecham-dry-bones-roundel.jpg`: Georg Pecham, *Ezekiel in the Valley of the Dry Bones*, pen and black ink with brown, blue and pink wash, c. 1600. The Met, 2005.26. [Museum object record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/366728).
- `ghisi-vision-of-ezekiel.jpg`: Giorgio Ghisi, *The Vision of Ezekiel*, 1554, engraving. The Met, 1994.297.3. Used to show the later Christian reading of Ezekiel 37 as bodily resurrection. [Museum object record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/359962).
- `ovile-christi-good-shepherd.jpg`: Anonymous, Netherlandish, *The Parable of the Good Shepherd* ("Ovile Christi"), c. 1570, etching on two sheets. The Met, 53.601.70. Pairs Ezekiel 34 with John 10. [Museum object record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/814733).
