# Daniel public-domain textbook artwork & artifacts

Retrieved 2026-10-10. JPEG originals are kept for the lightbox (`data-full`). WebP previews are resized without cropping.

- `rembrandt-belshazzar.jpg`: Rembrandt van Rijn, *Belshazzar's Feast*, 1635, oil on canvas, 167.6 × 209.2 cm; The National Gallery, London (NG1242). Depicts Daniel 5: King Belshazzar's terror as the divine hand inscribes the Aramaic words *Mene, Mene, Tekel, Upharsin* while golden vessels taken from the Jerusalem Temple are desecrated. Public domain. [National Gallery record](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/rembrandt-belshazzars-feast). [Wikimedia Commons file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rembrandt-Belsazar.jpg).
- `rubens-daniel-lions.jpg`: Peter Paul Rubens, *Daniel in the Lions' Den*, c. 1614–1616, oil on canvas, 224.2 × 330.5 cm; National Gallery of Art, Washington, D.C. (1965.13.1; Ailsa Mellon Bruce Fund). Illustrates Daniel 6: Daniel seated in prayer among ten lifelike lions, illuminated by the shaft of daylight from the sealed pit's opening above. Public domain / NGA Open Access. [NGA record](https://www.nga.gov/collection/art-object-page.50298.html). [Wikimedia Commons file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sir_Peter_Paul_Rubens_-_Daniel_in_the_Lions%27_Den_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).
- `cyrus-cylinder.jpg`: *The Cyrus Cylinder*, 539 BCE, clay barrel cylinder inscribed in Babylonian cuneiform, excavated at Babylon in 1879 by Hormuzd Rassam; British Museum, London (BM 90920). Details Cyrus the Great's conquest of Babylon and decree restoring exiled peoples and their sanctuaries, directly matching the historical context of Daniel 1:21, 6:28, and Daniel 9:1–2. Public domain. [British Museum record](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1880-0617-1941). [Wikimedia Commons file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cyrus_Cylinder_2.jpg).
- `babylon-lion-relief.jpg`: *Striding Lion Relief*, c. 604–562 BCE (reign of Nebuchadnezzar II), molded and glazed polychrome terracotta brick panel from the Processional Way leading to the Ishtar Gate of Babylon; Vorderasiatisches Museum / Pergamon Museum, Berlin. Represents the imperial power and symbolic visual landscape of Babylon encountered by Daniel and his companions (Daniel 1–4). Public domain. [Pergamon Museum / SMB record](https://recherche.smb.museum/detail/1744574). [Wikimedia Commons file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Babylon_processional_way.jpg).
- `nabonidus-cylinder.jpg`: *The Nabonidus Cylinder of Sippar*, c. 550 BCE, baked clay cylinder of the last king of Babylon, British Museum, London (BM 91128). Inscribes a prayer to the moon-god Sin naming Nabonidus's eldest son *Bel-shar-usur* (Belshazzar), confirming Belshazzar as crown prince and co-regent, explaining why Daniel was offered the position of "third ruler in the kingdom" (Daniel 5:7, 16, 29). Public domain. [British Museum record](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1882-0714-1025). [Wikimedia Commons file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cylinder_Nabonidus_BM_WA91128.jpg).
