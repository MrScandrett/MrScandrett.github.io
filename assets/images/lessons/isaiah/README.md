# Isaiah public-domain artwork & artifacts

Retrieved 2026-10-03 & 2026-10-10. JPEG originals are kept for the lightbox (`data-full`). WebP previews are resized without cropping.

- `michelangelo-isaiah.jpg`: Michelangelo, *Prophet Isaiah*, 1508–1512, Sistine Chapel ceiling. Public domain faithful reproduction (PD-Art). [Source and license](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jesaja_(Michelangelo).jpg).
- `dore-babylon.jpg`: Gustave Doré, *Isaiah’s Vision of the Destruction of Babylon*, 1866, Doré’s English Bible. Public domain faithful reproduction (PD-Art / PD-old-100-expired). [Source and license](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:121.Isaiah%27s_Vision_of_the_Destruction_of_Babylon.jpg).
- `hicks-peaceable-kingdom.jpg`: Edward Hicks, *Peaceable Kingdom*, ca. 1830–32. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 1970.283.1. Gift of Edgar William and Bernice Chrysler Garbisch, 1970. Public domain / Met Open Access CC0. [Museum record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/11081).
- `great-isaiah-scroll.jpg`: *The Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsa-a)*, c. 125 BCE, Dead Sea Scrolls found in Cave 1 at Qumran; The Israel Museum, Jerusalem (Shrine of the Book). The oldest virtually complete copy of any biblical book, preserving all 66 chapters of Isaiah on 54 parchment columns sewn together into a 7.34-meter scroll. Public domain / Israel Museum Open Access. [Israel Museum / Commons record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Great_Isaiah_Scroll.jpg).
- `sennacherib-prism.jpg`: *Sennacherib's Annals Prism (Taylor Prism)*, c. 691 BCE, hexagonal baked clay prism inscribed in Neo-Assyrian cuneiform; British Museum, London (WA 103000). Sennacherib records his 701 BCE campaign against Judah and King Hezekiah: *"As for Hezekiah the Judahite, who had not submitted to my yoke, I shut him up in Jerusalem, his royal city, like a caged bird in a cage."* Directly confirms the imperial siege and historical crisis narratively resolved in Isaiah 36–37 (and 2 Kings 18–19). Public domain. [British Museum record](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1855-1003-1). [Wikimedia Commons file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sennacherib_annals_prism_WA_103000.jpg).

Captions distinguish the passages from later artistic interpretations and connect primary archaeological witnesses directly to text references.
