AC vs. DC Electricity

Both are moving electric charge. The difference is direction: direct current keeps one polarity, while alternating current repeatedly reverses polarity. That simple distinction shapes batteries, outlets, motors, electronics, and the power grid.

Grades 7–12Interactive waveform labTransmission calculator~25 minutes

Start with the current

Electric current is the rate at which charge passes a point, measured in amperes (A). “AC” and “DC” describe how that current—and usually the voltage driving it—changes with time.

⎓

Direct current (DC)

Charge has one net direction. The voltage keeps the same polarity, though its value does not have to be perfectly constant.

  • Batteries and solar cells produce DC
  • Phones, computers, LEDs, and chips operate internally on DC
  • Shown as a flat line when the voltage is steady
∿

Alternating current (AC)

Charge reverses direction periodically. Utility AC is close to a sine wave and completes 60 cycles each second in North America.

  • Wall outlets and conventional power grids supply AC
  • Transformers easily change AC voltage
  • Described by frequency, amplitude, phase, and waveform
Electron motion is not energy delivery. In an AC wire, individual electrons mostly drift back and forth over tiny distances. Energy still travels from generator to load through the circuit’s electromagnetic field.

Important modern update: AC won early distribution because transformers made voltage conversion practical. Today, power electronics can efficiently convert DC voltage too. Modern grids use both: HVAC for broad networks and HVDC for selected long-distance, undersea, and grid-to-grid links.

Why high voltage reduces wire loss

For the same delivered power, raising transmission voltage lowers current: I = P ÷ V. Since resistive heating is Ploss = I²R, reducing current sharply reduces wasted energy. Compare two transmission voltages below.

Conversion is everywhere

🔌Phone charger

A rectifier and switching converter turn outlet AC into regulated low-voltage DC.

☀️Solar inverter

Solar panels make DC; an inverter synchronizes it as AC for a building or grid.

🚗Electric vehicle

The battery stores DC. Power electronics create controlled AC for many traction motors.

💻Computer

The power supply converts AC to several DC rails used by processors, memory, and drives.

🏭Variable-speed motor

A drive rectifies AC to DC, then synthesizes variable-frequency AC to control speed.

🌐HVDC link

Converter stations connect AC grids through a controllable high-voltage DC line.

Safety: Never experiment with a wall outlet or mains wiring. Both AC and DC can shock, burn, start fires, or stop the heart. Classroom circuit work should use current-limited, extra-low-voltage supplies under teacher supervision.

Tesla, Edison, and the modern grid

In the late 1800s, Edison’s systems distributed low-voltage DC. Westinghouse deployed practical AC systems, while Nikola Tesla’s polyphase patents and induction motor helped make AC useful for industry. The contest involved many engineers, inventors, financiers, patents, and public-safety campaigns—not just two lone rivals.

AC became the foundation of public power systems because transformers could efficiently raise voltage for transmission and lower it near users. That history does not mean DC “lost forever”: batteries, electronics, solar arrays, EVs, data centers, and HVDC transmission make DC essential today.
