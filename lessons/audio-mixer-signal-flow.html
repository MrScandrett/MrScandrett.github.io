✨ FX Bus — Reverb / Delay

Pre-fader vs. post-fader sends AUX 1 (Monitor) is tapped pre-fader — right after the preamp — so a performer's stage monitor mix doesn't jump around when you ride their channel fader. AUX 2 (FX) is tapped post-fader, so the reverb tail rises and falls together with the dry vocal, the way it should.
Why a shared FX bus? Instead of giving every channel its own reverb, the console sends a little of each channel to one shared reverb via AUX 2, then blends that single wet signal back in with the FX Return knob on the master strip. Start the return low — it's easy to add more, hard to un-hear too much.

Know Your Connectors

XLR — 3 pins, balanced, locking

The standard microphone cable. Two copies of the signal (one flipped) cancel out noise picked up along the run, and the latch keeps it from pulling loose mid-show. Use it for every mic input.

TRS — Tip · Ring · Sleeve, balanced

A ¼″ jack with two dark rings. Used for balanced line-level gear (keyboards, DI-free synths) and for balanced main outs feeding powered speakers.

TS — Tip · Sleeve, unbalanced

A ¼″ jack with one dark ring. The standard instrument cable for a guitar or bass plugged straight into a Hi-Z input. Keep these runs short — they pick up hum.

RCA — stereo consumer pair

Red/white pair, unbalanced. Common for feeding a phone, tablet, or media player into an aux/tape input — never for a mic or a main speaker feed.

Bare speaker wire — power, not signal

Carries amplified current from a power amplifier to a passive speaker. It is not a line-level connector — plugging it straight into a mixer's main out sends almost no usable signal and can damage the output.

Setting Up the Mixer Yourself

🧰 Step-by-Step Setup Checklist

  1. With everything powered off, patch each source into the correct input type: XLR for mics, TS for instruments, TRS for balanced line gear, RCA for consumer players.
  2. Patch the main outs to your speakers or amp with balanced TRS/XLR — never bare speaker wire from a mixer's line output.
  3. Pull every channel fader and the master fader down to minimum, and every gain knob to zero.
  4. Power on the mixer first, then your amplifiers/powered speakers last. Power off in the reverse order.
  5. Turn on phantom power only for channels with condenser mics that require it.
  6. One channel at a time, bring up gain while the performer plays/sings at full volume, until the channel meter reads just under clipping.
  7. Bring the channel fader up to unity (0 dB) before slowly bringing up the master fader.
  8. Set AUX sends last: Monitor (AUX 1) to taste for the performer, FX (AUX 2) sparingly — reverb is easy to overdo.

🖐 Real Hardware Habits

On a physical console every control is a knob or a fader, not a checkbox — you learn its feel with your hand, not your eyes. That's why the knobs here rotate and the faders slide vertically: drag up/down on a knob to turn it, drag the fader cap along its track, and both also respond to arrow keys once focused.

Real techs scan a console left to right, channel 1 through 12, the same way every time — the numbered labels above each strip are there so that habit starts now.

🎚 Gain Staging & Unity

Set gain (trim) so the loudest moment of the source sits just below clipping at the preamp — that's your headroom. Leave the fader near unity (0 dB), where the channel neither boosts nor cuts. Mixing happens with faders; gain is set once per performance and left alone.

Golden rule: if the preamp clips, no fader below it can undo the damage. Fix problems at the earliest stage they appear.

🔀 AUX Sends: Pre-Fader vs. Post-Fader

A pre-fader send taps the signal before the channel fader — used for monitor mixes, so a performer's in-ear mix stays steady even while you're riding their fader out front.

A post-fader send taps the signal after the fader — used for effects like reverb and delay, so the wet effect rises and falls together with the dry signal instead of getting out of proportion when you pull a fader down.

⚡ Phantom Power Safety

+48V phantom power is only needed for condenser microphones — most dynamic mics (like an SM58) and all instrument/line inputs don't need it. Turning it on for a channel with nothing connected is safe; plugging or unplugging an unbalanced cable while phantom power is live can cause a loud pop, so patch first, power second.

🔁 Feedback in 10 Seconds

Feedback is a loop: the mic hears the speaker, the speaker replays the mic, and one frequency snowballs into a howl. Break the loop by moving the mic behind the speakers, pointing it away from them, or cutting the ringing frequency on the EQ. Turning everything down is the last resort, not the first.
