Lighting Design Essentials

🔴🟢🔵 Light Adds Up

Stage lights mix additively — the opposite of paint. Red + green light makes yellow, green + blue makes cyan, red + blue makes magenta, and all three at full power make white. Try it on the sliders: start at zero and build colors up.

🔥❄️ Warm vs. Cool

When a director asks for a "warm" scene, they mean amber, gold, and soft pink — colors that read as sunlight, candlelight, comfort, and energy. "Cool" means blues and lavenders — night, moonlight, sadness, distance. The same set can feel like a summer afternoon or a lonely midnight with nothing changed but the color of the light.

🧭 Direction Tells the Story

Front light makes faces visible — necessary, but flat on its own. Side light sculpts: it carves out bodies in space, which is why dance is lit almost entirely from the sides. Back light separates the performer from the background with a rim of light — kill the front light and it becomes pure silhouette, the most dramatic look on stage.

🎚 Intensity Is Time of Day

A wash at 100% feels like noon; the same color at 40% feels like evening. Brightness sets energy: full intensity for a dance number, a dim pool of light for a confession. If a scene feels wrong and the color is right, try the intensity before anything else.

🎬 Cues Are Storytelling

A lighting cue is a saved "look" — every fixture's color and level — that plays back on the word GO. Shows are a stack of cues fired in order, each fade timed to the scene. A slow 5-second sunset fade and a snap blackout tell completely different stories with the same two looks.

Production Design Briefs

🗞 Brief 1 — Newsboy Energy

It's 1899 New York at dawn and the newsboys are waking up the city. Build a look that says grit, hope, and hustle — think warm but not cozy. Save it as a cue named "Carrying the Banner."

✊ Brief 2 — Revolution

The strike begins. What RGB values say defiance? Design the biggest, boldest look you can, then decide: does the back light make them heroes or shadows? Save it as "The World Will Know."

🍫 Brief 3 — Pure Imagination

Now flip worlds completely: the gates of the Chocolate Factory swing open. Candy-colored, saturated, magical — nothing that could ever appear in 1899 New York. Save it as "The Factory Reveal."

🏚 Brief 4 — The Bucket Shack

Poverty, cold, and love in a broken little house. How little color can still be beautiful? Dim, grey-blue, one fixture doing most of the work. Save it as "Bucket Shack," then GO between it and "The Factory Reveal" — that contrast is the whole show.

🌇 Brief 5 — Run Your Show

Put all four cues in order and press GO through the sequence. Watch the fades: does each transition serve the story? A designer's job isn't pretty pictures — it's the journey between them.
