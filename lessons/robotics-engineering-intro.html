Before we touch anything

Lab rules & circuit safety

Robots run on electricity, and electricity deserves respect. These rules apply every session, on every bench.

Power off to build

Disconnect USB or battery power before you change any wiring. Build first, power second.

Low voltage only

Everything on your bench runs on 5 volts or less from USB or batteries — never wall outlets or mains wiring.

Check before power-up

Trace your circuit with your finger before connecting power. Look for short circuits: bare wires touching that shouldn't.

One hand rule

When you do test a live circuit, keep one hand in your pocket. It's an old electrician's habit that keeps current from ever crossing your chest.

Components have a "wrong way"

LEDs, capacitors, and chips are polarized. If something gets warm fast or smells odd, disconnect power immediately.

Ask before you're unsure

A confused circuit is a normal Tuesday. A smoking one is not. When in doubt, hands off and ask.

A thought experiment

Is a webpage a robot?

Your student website doesn't have motors or LEDs — so is it fair to call it a robot? Compare the parts before you decide.

Robot partPhysical robot exampleWebpage equivalent
SensorTouch sensor, camera, microphoneA click, a keystroke, a form field, a scroll
ControllerArduino Uno running a sketchJavaScript running in the browser
ActuatorMotor, LED, speakerUpdating text on screen, changing a color, playing a sound
Feedback loopSense → decide → act → sense againAn event listener waits, fires, then waits again for the next input

Sort of — and that "sort of" is the point. A webpage shares the exact sense → think → act loop every robot uses. What it's missing is a physical actuator: it can't push a real object or move through real space on its own.

Engineers call this distinction embodiment — whether a system acts on the physical world or only on information. Your student website is where you'll practice the controller half of robotics all year: reading input, making decisions, and producing a response — even before you ever plug in an Arduino.
