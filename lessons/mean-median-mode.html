Mathematics · Lesson 11

Mean, Median & Mode

Mean = the average Median = the middle value Mode = the most frequent value

Mean, median, and mode all try to describe a "typical" value in a dataset, but they respond very differently to outliers. In this lesson, students drag dots on a number line and watch all three measures recompute live, including a demo of how one extreme value skews the mean but barely touches the median.

Learning Target

I can calculate the mean, median, and mode of a dataset and explain how outliers affect each one.

By the end of the lesson, students should be able to compute all three measures of center, and explain why the median is more resistant to outliers than the mean.

Three Measures of Center

Each measure summarizes a dataset differently, and each can tell a different story about the same numbers.

MeanAdd every value, then divide by how many values there are. Sensitive to outliers.
MedianSort the values and find the middle one (or average the two middle values). Resistant to outliers.
ModeThe value that appears most often. A dataset can have one mode, several, or none.

Worked Example

Find the mean, median, and mode of the dataset 4, 7, 7, 9, 3.

Mean = (4+7+7+9+3) / 5
Add all five values, then divide by 5.
Mean = 30 / 5 = 6
The average of the five values is 6.
Sorted: 3, 4, 7, 7, 9
Arrange the values from least to greatest to find the median.
Median = 7
With five values, the middle (3rd) value is the median.
Mode = 7
7 appears twice, more than any other value.

Exit Ticket

Students answer these in complete sentences or with shown work.

  • A team's scores are 10, 12, 11, 9, 58. Why is the median a better description of a "typical" score than the mean here?
  • Create a dataset of five numbers where the mean, median, and mode are all different.
  • Explain, in your own words, why the mean changes a lot when you add an outlier but the median usually does not.
Definitions
Mean sum of values divided by the count.
Median the middle value in sorted order.
Mode the most frequent value.
Common Trap
Outliers pull the mean toward them but barely move the median — that's why income and home-price data usually report medians.
Lesson Flow
Warm-up Ask students to guess the "average" test score from a list you read aloud.
Mini lesson Model finding all three measures for a small dataset.
Practice Use "Add Outlier Demo" to show how much the mean shifts vs. the median.
Check Use the exit ticket to confirm students can justify which measure to use.
