2. Find the room nodes

Node

A fixed location where one standing-wave mode has minimum pressure variation. At that frequency, bass may seem unusually weak—but the location is not silent at every frequency.

Antinode

A location where that mode has maximum pressure variation. The same bass note may sound much louder here, especially near rigid room boundaries.

Room mode

A resonance formed when reflected waves repeatedly return in phase. Axial modes involve two opposing surfaces; tangential and oblique modes involve four or six.

f(nₓ,nᵧ,nz) = c / 2 × √[(nₓ/L)² + (nᵧ/W)² + (nz/H)²]

The top-down canvas selects the nearest horizontal mode, where nz = 0. Floor-to-ceiling modes still exist, but require a vertical section to see their nodes.

Choose Room modes / nodes in the simulation view, then sweep the test frequency.
  1. Move the source onto a dark node. That mode is excited less strongly.
  2. Move the listener between a node and bright antinode. Compare the predicted pressure.
  3. Change room dimensions. Larger distances between walls produce lower modal frequencies.

4. Field measurement

  1. Stand safely near the room center and make one sharp clap or use an approved impulse.
  2. Record it from a fixed position. Observe how quickly the decay disappears into the noise floor.
  3. Repeat with curtains open/closed and with the class present/absent.
  4. Explain why audience members are acoustic treatment—even when they are quiet.

Safety: never create dangerously loud impulses, and never aim a loudspeaker directly at someone at close range.

Room controls

The Sabine model

RT60 = 0.161 × V / A

V is room volume in cubic metres. A is total equivalent absorption area in sabins. RT60 estimates how long sound takes to decay by 60 dB.

Speech usually benefits from a shorter decay than orchestral music. A useful target depends on room volume, purpose, background noise, and standards—not one magic number.

Diagnose before buying

Echo or reverberation?

An echo arrives as a distinct repeat. Reverberation is a dense tail of many reflections.

Absorb or diffuse?

Absorption removes reflected energy. Diffusion scatters it in many directions to avoid strong, focused reflections.

Source first

Moving a microphone closer and aiming speakers at listeners can improve direct sound before expensive treatment.
