Lesson 06 · 75 minutes · Motion design

Pose the story. Then time it.

Create two game-ready actions: an idle that communicates character and a walk that loops cleanly. Explore how the same rig moves in naturalistic, cartoon, anime-inspired, and low-poly styles—then choose a motion language that fits your game.

01 / PLAN

Animation brief

Describe the motion before adding keys.

Idle purpose Show breathing, alertness, weight, and personality without distracting the player.
Walk purpose Communicate speed, mass, attitude, and reliable locomotion.
Frame rate Set a stable scene rate, then decide whether motion plays smoothly, on twos, or with intentional stepped holds.
Style brief Name the visual language—naturalistic, cartoon, anime-inspired, retro, or a deliberate hybrid—and define how far poses should be pushed.
Root motion For this lesson, animate in place so Godot controls world movement.
Loop contract The final displayed pose must flow into the first without a visible hitch.
02 / IDLE

Controlled stillness

Create life without constant noise.

  1. Create and name an Action: Idle.Use the Dope Sheet’s Action Editor and protect intentional actions from accidental loss.
  2. Key the first pose.Shift weight slightly, relax shoulders, angle head, and keep feet planted. With the posed bones selected, press I and choose Rotation (or LocRotScale for the root bone) to set the keyframe.
  3. Add a middle extreme.Raise the chest, offset the hands, and change the line of action. Keep it subtle for naturalism or push the contrast for a graphic style.
  4. Return to the first pose.Copy the initial keyed pose to the loop end.
  5. Offset secondary motion.Let chest, head, and hands reach their extremes on slightly different frames.
Watch the feet: If the character is meant to stand still, feet must not slide. Locking planted contacts is more important than adding extra movement.
03 / WALK

Four-pose structure

Build the walk from readable extremes.

CONTACT

Opposite limbs reach

Front heel and back toe meet the ground; arms oppose the legs.

DOWN

Weight lands

Body reaches its lowest point; front foot settles and limbs bend.

PASSING

Back leg passes

Support leg straightens while the free foot travels under the hips.

UP

Body rises

Heel lifts and the body reaches its highest point before the next contact.

Pose one half-cycle, copy it to the second half, then mirror the pose. Do not judge the walk from one angle—check front, side, and perspective views.

STYLE / RANGE

One rig, different motion languages

Style changes the rules—not the need for clarity.

A convincing walk does not have one universal shape. Match exaggeration, spacing, and playback cadence to the game’s art direction.

NATURALISTIC

Observed weight

Subtle rise and fall, moderate reach, smooth spacing, and restrained overlap support cinematic or realistic characters.

24–30 fps · smooth curves
CARTOON

Graphic exaggeration

Push the high and low poses, open the silhouette, and let anticipation and follow-through carry personality.

24 fps · broad arcs
ANIME-INSPIRED

Designed accents

Hold readable drawings, then snap through transitions. Spend frames on the pose that communicates the idea.

12–24 fps · held accents
LOW-POLY RETRO

Purposeful limitation

Use fewer sampled poses, crisp changes, and a compact silhouette that remains readable at gameplay distance.

8–12 fps · stepped timing
Do not confuse style with quality: Limited animation can be highly intentional, while smooth animation can still feel weak. Judge whether the chosen motion language is consistent and readable.

04 / POLISH

Curves serve the poses

Fix timing, spacing, arcs, and contact.

  1. Playblast or preview on loop.Watch at real speed, then frame-step through the hitch.
  2. Open the Graph Editor.Smooth unwanted bumps for continuous motion, or preserve deliberate holds and sharp transitions for limited and anime-inspired timing.
  3. Track arcs.Hands, head, hips, and feet should travel through deliberate curves rather than jittering.
  4. Remove foot sliding.During planted frames, the foot’s world-space position should remain stable.
  5. Check the loop boundary.Do not render duplicate first and last poses as two held frames; configure the action range cleanly.

05 / IMPORT

Actions into Godot

Export motion as named, testable clips.

  1. Keep Actions clearly named.Use Idle and Walk; remove or rename test actions that should not ship.
  2. Confirm deformation-only export.Do not export unnecessary control helpers as deform bones.
  3. Export GLB with animation enabled.Keep the mesh, armature, and intended actions selected and included.
  4. Inspect Godot’s advanced import settings.Confirm animation names, loop behavior, and sampling. Reimport after changes.
  5. Play both clips in an AnimationPlayer or AnimationTree.Check scale, pose, speed, looping, root behavior, and deformation.
Character jumps or rotates on import? Check unapplied armature/mesh transforms, root-bone animation, and whether world translation was keyed accidentally. Fix the source and re-export instead of patching every imported instance.
