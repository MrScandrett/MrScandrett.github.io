Mathematics · Lesson 10

Area & Perimeter Playground

Area = space inside a shape Perimeter = distance around a shape Same perimeter, different area

Area and perimeter measure two different things about the same shape. In this lesson, students click cells on a grid to build shapes and watch area and perimeter update instantly, then take on a challenge to build shapes with equal perimeter but different area.

Learning Target

I can find the area and perimeter of a shape built from grid squares and explain what each measures.

By the end of the lesson, students should be able to count filled squares to find area, count outer edges to find perimeter, and explain why two shapes can share a perimeter but have very different areas.

Area vs. Perimeter

Area measures how much surface a shape covers. Perimeter measures the distance around its outside edge.

AreaCount the unit squares that make up the shape. For a rectangle, area = length × width.
PerimeterCount every outer edge of the shape. For a rectangle, perimeter = 2 × (length + width).

Worked Example

Find the area and perimeter of a rectangle that is 4 units long and 3 units wide.

Area = 4 × 3
Multiply length by width to count the squares inside.
Area = 12 square units
Twelve unit squares fit inside the rectangle.
Perimeter = 2 × (4 + 3)
Add length and width, then double it for all four sides.
Perimeter = 14 units
Walking all the way around the rectangle covers 14 units.

Exit Ticket

Students answer these in complete sentences or with shown work.

  • Build two shapes with the same perimeter but different areas. Sketch both and label the area of each.
  • A garden is 8 feet by 3 feet. How much fencing is needed to go around it, and how much ground does it cover?
  • Explain, in your own words, the difference between area and perimeter.
Formulas
Area length × width, measured in square units.
Perimeter 2 × (length + width), measured in units.
Common Trap
Same perimeter does not guarantee the same area — a long thin rectangle can have far less area than a square with the same perimeter.
Lesson Flow
Warm-up Ask students which takes longer: walking around a room or covering its floor with tiles.
Mini lesson Model counting squares for area and edges for perimeter on the grid.
Practice Have students race to build a shape matching a target perimeter.
Check Use the exit ticket to confirm students separate the two ideas.
