Before someone reads a single sentence, their brain has already mapped the page. Good typography gives readers effortless mental milestones.

Good typography tells a reader where to look first, second, and third. Size, weight, and the space between lines do that work: the headline is biggest, the subhead is smaller, and the body text is set so a long paragraph is easy to read.

How type guides the eye

1. The Problem: The Visual Wall of Noise

Imagine opening a book or newspaper where every word had the exact same size, bold thickness, and line spacing. Your eyes wouldn't know where to begin, what was important, or when one thought ended and another began.

3. The Mathematics of Modular Scales

Just like musical scales are built on frequency ratios (octaves 2:1, fifths 3:2), graphic designers build typographic scales by multiplying a base size (usually 16 px) by a chosen ratio. These values update with your design.
