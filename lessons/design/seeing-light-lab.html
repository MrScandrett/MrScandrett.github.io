Chapter 1 · Seeing & Light (Grades K–2)

Seeing Light Lab

Vision starts with light. Discover how your eyes catch light rays, how brightness helps us see edges, and how simple shapes combine into the world around us.

1

How Your Eye Catches Light

The black dot in the center of your eye is called the pupil. It is a tiny doorway that opens wide in the dark to let in every bit of light, and squeezes small in bright sunlight so too much light doesn't hurt your eye!

🌙 Moonlight: The pupil grows BIG to catch dim light!
2

Light Makes Shapes Appear

In pitch darkness, your eyes cannot see objects because there is no light bouncing off them. Move the brightness slider to see how light reveals edges and colors!

🦉
Can you spot the friendly owl in the dark?
🌑 ☀️
3

Simple Shapes Make Big Pictures

Designers and artists don't draw complicated things all at once. Our brains recognize objects by spotting simple geometric shapes: circles, triangles, and rectangles!

Shapes used: 1 Circle (head) + 2 Triangles (ears) + Dots (eyes).

🌟 What We Learned Today

  • Pupils adapt: Big in the dark to collect light; small in bright sun to protect your eye.
  • Light reveals contrast: We see things when light bounces off an object and into our eyes.
  • Shapes tell stories: Triangles, circles, and squares are the building blocks that artists use to make pictures everyone recognizes!
