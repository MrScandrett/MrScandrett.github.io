BRIEF

Studio challenge

Create one believable corner of a game.

Pick a clear art direction: cozy spaceship cabin, abandoned research lab, stylized bedroom, fantasy workshop, or another teacher-approved idea. Reuse the same palette, proportions, and level of detail across every lesson.

DESIGN RULE

One visual language

Decide whether forms are rounded or sharp, surfaces are clean or worn, and proportions are realistic or exaggerated.

TECH RULE

One dependable pipeline

Save source files separately from exported files. Test the exported GLB, not only the scene in your 3D app.

Folder + source rule: Use asset-studio/source for your app's own files (.blend, .ma, .max, .skp, .kra, .psd…), asset-studio/textures for images, asset-studio/exports for .glb files, and asset-studio/reference for source records. Use your own work or verified CC0/public-domain textures, HDRIs, skyboxes, and models—never generated or ambiguously licensed assets.

ANY APP

Bring your own software

Make it anywhere. Polish it here. Ship it everywhere.

These lessons teach the craft, not one program. Model in Blender, Maya, 3ds Max, SketchUp, Tinkercad, Blockbench or Nomad; paint in Krita, GIMP, Photoshop, Photopea, Procreate or Aseprite; rig with Mixamo or by hand. Whatever you use, and whatever you learn next, the work moves through the same three stations:

  1. Makein the app you know. Keep its own file as your source.
  2. BenchDrop the export on the Asset Bench. It checks against your brief and fixes the common export accidents.
  3. EngineBring the clean .glb or .png into Godot, Unity, Unreal, Roblox Studio or Three.js. It can also go back into your app.
