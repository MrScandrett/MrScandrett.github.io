Glossary

Bowing (right hand)
ArcoPlayed with the bow — the default, unless a piece says otherwise.
Pizzicato (pizz.)Plucked with a finger instead of bowed, until "arco" returns.
DétachéSmooth, separate bow strokes — one note per bow direction.
LegatoNotes slurred smoothly under one continuous bow stroke.
StaccatoShort, separated notes, usually on one bow but stopped between.
SpiccatoA bouncing bow stroke where the bow leaves the string.
TremoloVery fast, unmeasured back-and-forth strokes on one note.
Sul ponticello / sul tastoBowing near the bridge (glassy) or over the fingerboard (hollow).
Left hand & pitch
PositionWhere the whole left hand sits on the neck.
ShiftingSliding the hand from one position to another.
VibratoA small, controlled pitch oscillation from rocking the finger.
Glissando (gliss.)An audible slide between two pitches — try it on the fingerboard.
Double stopTwo strings stopped and bowed together, sounding two notes.
HarmonicA bell-like overtone from touching a string lightly, not pressing.
IntonationHow accurately a stopped note matches its true pitch.
Open stringPlayed with no finger down; a tuning reference for nearby notes.
Instrument & setup
BridgeThe upright piece strings pass over, driving the body's vibration.
SoundpostA dowel inside the body, under the bridge, carrying vibration to the back.
F-holesThe f-shaped openings that let the body's resonant sound escape.
Fine tunersSmall tailpiece screws for precise pitch adjustment.
RosinResin rubbed on the bow hair so it grips the string.
Chin/shoulder restSupports that balance the violin, freeing the left hand.
