2 · Why Is It Always True? (Interactive Proofs)

Testing one 3-4-5 triangle only shows an example. A proof guarantees a² + b² = c² for every right triangle. Notice the shapes below resize live with your Blueprint Lab sliders.

a² + b² ↔ c²
Outer Box (a+b)² 49
4 Triangles (2ab) 24
Leftover Space (Box − 4 Triangles) 25

Both layouts fill the exact same (a + b) × (a + b) outer square using four identical right triangles (each of area ½ab):

Layout 1 (Two leg-squares + 4 triangles)
Area = a² + b² + 4(½ab)
(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
(3 + 4)² = 9 + 24 + 16 = 49
Layout 2 (Tilted c² square + 4 triangles)
Area = c² + 4(½ab)
(a + b)² = c² + 2ab
(3 + 4)² = 25 + 24 = 49
Cancel 2ab from both expressions: since both equal (a + b)², subtracting the four triangles (2ab) from each side leaves a² + b² = c².

3 · Why 90° Matters — Angle-Hinge & Converse Lab

Keep two legs fixed (a = 3 and b = 4) and open or close the hinge angle γ between them. The equation a² + b² = c² is an exact test for whether a triangle has a right angle.

90°
Right Triangle (90°)
a² + b² = c² (25.0 = 25.0)
a² + b² (fixed)25.0
c² (changes with γ)25.0
Right Triangle (90°) a² + b² = c². The Pythagorean theorem holds exactly.
Acute Triangle (< 90°) All angles are under 90°. Closing the hinge shrinks c, so a² + b² > c².
Obtuse Triangle (> 90°) One angle exceeds 90°. Opening the hinge stretches c, so a² + b² < c².

4 · The Formula, Solving Steps & Famous Triples

In any right triangle, the two sides forming the 90° angle are the legs (a and b). The longest side across from 90° is the hypotenuse (c).

a2 + b2 = c2

Finding the Hypotenuse (c)

c = √(a² + b²)

  1. 1Square both legs: calculate a² and b².
  2. 2Add them together: a² + b².
  3. 3Take the square root to get c.

Finding a Missing Leg (a or b)

a = √(c² − b²)

  1. 1Square the hypotenuse c² and known leg b².
  2. 2Subtract the known leg square: c² − b².
  3. 3Take the square root to get the missing leg.
Common Mistake Check: Never add c² + b² when looking for a missing leg—that would make the leg longer than the hypotenuse! Always check that c remains the single longest side.

6 · Before Pythagoras: A Global Discovery

Although the theorem bears the name of Pythagoras of Samos (c. 570–495 BCE), clay tablets from Old Babylon (such as Plimpton 322 and YBC 7289, c. 1800 BCE) list whole-number Pythagorean triples and compute √2 more than a thousand years earlier. Builders in ancient Egypt used 12-knot ropes in a 3-4-5 loop to lay square foundations, while scholars in India (Sulba Sutras) and China (Zhoubi Suanjing) independently stated and proved the relationship.

Later writers credited Pythagoras or his school with a proof, though surviving sources do not confirm who gave the first proof. The earliest surviving rigorous proof appears in Euclid's Elements (Book I, Proposition 47, c. 300 BCE)—the same altitude-splitting idea you can toggle in the Blueprint Lab above. Exit Ticket · Why does a² + b² = c² only hold true when there is a 90° angle?

As you saw in the Angle-Hinge Lab, changing the angle between legs a and b stretches or shrinks the third side c even when a and b stay fixed. At < 90°, a² + b² > c²; at > 90°, a² + b² < c². Only at 90° does the rearrangement proof hold and a² + b² = c².
