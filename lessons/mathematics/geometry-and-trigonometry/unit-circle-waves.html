The Unit Circle & Waves

Unit circle Radians SOH-CAH-TOA Sine waves Toward Fourier

Spin a point around a circle and its height traces a wave. That single picture is trigonometry: triangles, rotation, sound, light, and every repeating pattern in nature are the same idea seen from different sides. Build it with your hands, then use it.

Learning Targets

  • Read cosine as the x-coordinate and sine as the y-coordinate of a point on the unit circle.
  • Explain why sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 using the Pythagorean theorem.
  • Convert between degrees and radians and say what a radian actually measures.
  • Use SOH-CAH-TOA to find a missing side of a right triangle.
  • Write a wave as y = A sin(B(x − C)) + D and say what each letter does.

Prerequisites: Pythagoras & Triangles and Pi & Circles.

Why sin² + cos² = 1

The point, its shadow on the x-axis, and the origin make a right triangle. Its legs are cos θ and sin θ. Its hypotenuse is the radius, which is 1. Pythagoras says cos²θ + sin²θ = 1². Try any angle: the last readout never leaves 1.

Lab 2 · What Is a Radian?

Degrees are a human choice (360 came from the Babylonians). Radians come from the circle itself: one radian is the angle whose arc is exactly as long as the radius. Slide the angle and watch radius-length pieces peel off the arc onto the ruler below. The full circle takes a little more than six of them: 2π ≈ 6.28.

Degrees57.3°

The conversion: a half circle is π radians = 180°, so radians = degrees × π/180 and degrees = radians × 180/π. Arc length is simply s = rθ, with no extra constants, which is why science and engineering use radians.

Lab 3 · SOH-CAH-TOA

Any right triangle is a scaled copy of a unit-circle triangle. That is why the ratios of its sides depend only on the angle, not the size. Change the angle and the hypotenuse; the ratios barely notice the size.

Opposite4.59
Adjacent6.55
Hypotenuse8
Highlighted ratio0.574

Use it: to find a side, pick the ratio that contains the side you know and the side you want, then solve. Opposite = hyp × sin θ. Adjacent = hyp × cos θ. Opposite = adjacent × tan θ.

Lab 4 · Build a Wave

Every repeating pattern can be written as y = A sin(B(x − C)) + D. Each letter changes one visible thing:

  • A amplitude: how tall the wave is.
  • B frequency: how many cycles fit in 2π (period = 2π ÷ B).
  • C phase shift: slides the wave sideways.
  • D midline: slides the wave up or down.

Press “New target wave”, then match the dashed wave with your four sliders.

Lab 5 · Waves Add Up (a Preview of Fourier)

Here is the surprise that makes trig matter: add sine waves of different sizes and you can build almost any shape. Start with the recipe below and watch a square wave appear from nothing but curves.

Each slider is one harmonic: sin x, sin 3x, sin 5x, sin 7x. Add more of them and the corners get sharper. Continue this idea to infinity and you have the Fourier Series, which powers audio compression, JPEG, and every equalizer.

Where Trigonometry Lives

  • Games and graphics: rotating a sprite by θ uses (cos θ, sin θ).
  • Sound: a pure tone is a sine wave; pitch is B, loudness is A.
  • Surveying and navigation: tan θ turns an angle and a distance into a height.
  • Engineering and physics: springs, pendulums, AC power, and light are all waves.

Practice

Round to two decimal places where needed. Answers within 0.05 count.

1. What is sin 30°?

2. A ramp is 10 m long and rises at 30°. How high does it reach (m)?

3. An 8 m ladder leans against a wall at 60° to the ground. How high up the wall does it touch (m)?

4. The Sun is 45° above the horizon and a tree casts a 20 m shadow. How tall is the tree (m)?

5. In quadrant I, cos θ = 0.6. What is sin θ?

6. Convert 3π/2 radians to degrees.

7. What is the period of y = sin(2x)? (Give a decimal.)

Exit Ticket

  • Explain in a sentence why the point at 90° has cos θ = 0 and sin θ = 1.
  • A wave has amplitude 3, completes 2 cycles in 2π, and its midline is y = 1. Write its equation.
  • Why is a radian a more “natural” unit than a degree?
