SOH-CAH-TOA

Color-coded sides Sine, Cosine & Tangent Clinometer Lab Interactive practice

Three ratios. One right triangle. Find a missing length or angle by choosing the two sides that matter.

1 · Side Names 2 · SOH-CAH-TOA 3 · Solve Sides & Angles 3.5 · Clinometer Lab 4 · Practice 5 · Exit Ticket

1 · Name the sides from your angle

First check for a 90° angle. Choose one of the acute angles and call it θ (theta). The Hypotenuse is across from 90° and is always the longest side. The Opposite side is across from θ. The Adjacent side touches θ and is not the hypotenuse.

■ Opposite (across from θ) ■ Adjacent (next to θ) ■ Hypotenuse (across from 90°)

Predict: If you switch θ to the other acute angle, which two colors on the triangle will swap? Test it above.

Explain: The hypotenuse stays fixed; opposite and adjacent swap whenever your reference angle moves to the other acute corner (90° − θ). That is why sin(θ) = cos(90° − θ)—the side opposite θ is the side adjacent to (90° − θ)!

2 · Match the two sides to a ratio

SOH-CAH-TOA is a memory aid. Read each three-letter group as a trig function followed by a color-coded fraction:

SOH

sin θ = opposite ÷ hypotenuse

CAH

cos θ = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse

TOA

tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent

Choose by sides, not by guessing. Mark the known side and the wanted side. O + H means sine; A + H means cosine; O + A means tangent. Ignore the unused third side.

3 · Find a side or an angle

Use your calculator in degree mode (DEG). Write the ratio, substitute known values, rearrange, then calculate. Keep extra digits until the final answer.

Missing numerator: multiply

θ = 30°, hypotenuse = 12 cm. Find opposite.

sin 30° = x / 12
x = 12 × sin 30° = 6 cm

Missing denominator: divide

θ = 60°, adjacent = 5 cm. Find hypotenuse.

cos 60° = 5 / h
h × cos 60° = 5
h = 5 / cos 60° = 10 cm

Missing angle: use inverse trig

Opposite = 3 cm, adjacent = 4 cm. Find θ.

tan θ = 3 / 4
θ = tan⁻¹(3 / 4) ≈ 36.9°

The inverse function asks “which angle has this ratio?” On a calculator, use SHIFT/2nd + tan. Here tan⁻¹ means inverse tangent, not 1 / tan.

Sense check: Acute angles lie between 0° and 90°. The hypotenuse must be longer than either leg. Sine and cosine are between 0 and 1; tangent exceeds 1 whenever θ > 45°.

3.5 · Clinometer & Indirect Measurement Lab

How do surveyors, foresters, and aerospace engineers measure a landmark they cannot climb? They stand a horizontal distance d (adjacent) away, sight the top through a clinometer to read the angle of elevation θ, and use TOA (tan θ = rise / d) to find the vertical rise (opposite) above eye level h₀.

Rise above eye (d · tan θ) 57.6 m
Total height H = h₀ + rise 59.2 m
Line-of-sight distance 73.1 m

Pro tip: If you walk until your clinometer reads θ = 45°, then tan(45°) = 1, so the vertical rise equals your horizontal distance d without needing a calculator!

4 · Choose → solve → explain

Work through six challenges: ratios, missing sides, then missing angles. Sketch each right triangle and color-code Opposite, Adjacent, and Hypotenuse. Round calculated answers to one decimal place.

5 · Exit ticket

A wheelchair ramp rises 1.2 m vertically over a horizontal run of 4 m. Which ratio finds its angle θ to the ground? Write the equation before using a calculator.

Compare your reasoning

The vertical rise is opposite to θ and the horizontal run is adjacent. Use TOA: tan θ = 1.2 / 4 = 0.3. Then θ = tan⁻¹(0.3) ≈ 16.7°. The sloped ramp surface itself is the hypotenuse (√(1.2² + 4²) ≈ 4.18 m).

Color-Coded Method
1. Anchor on 90° & θ Across from 90° is always the Hypotenuse. Across from your chosen acute angle θ is Opposite; the remaining leg touching θ is Adjacent.
2. Pick the ratio by side pair • O & H → SOH (sin θ = O / H)
• A & H → CAH (cos θ = A / H)
• O & A → TOA (tan θ = O / A)
Key Identities
Co-functions sin(θ) = cos(90° − θ) because switching to the complementary angle swaps Opposite and Adjacent.
45° Benchmark In an isosceles right triangle (45°-45°-90°), Opposite = Adjacent, so tan(45°) = 1.
Classroom Facilitation
Common Misconception Students often treat “Opposite” and “Adjacent” as fixed positions (vertical vs. horizontal). Have them toggle Reference angle θ in Section 1 and watch the blue and orange segments trade places.
Clinometer Activity Pair Section 3.5 with a physical straw-and-protractor clinometer outdoors: measure d with a trundle wheel, sight the flagpole or roofline, and verify H = h₀ + d · tan θ.
