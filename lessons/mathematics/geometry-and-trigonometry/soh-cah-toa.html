Mathematics · Right-triangle trigonometry · Grades 8–10

SOH-CAH-TOA

Three ratios. One right triangle. Find a missing length or angle by choosing the two sides that matter.

1 · Name the sides from your angle

First check for a 90° angle. Choose one of the other angles and call it θ (theta). The hypotenuse is across from 90° and is always the longest side. The opposite side is across from θ. The adjacent side touches θ and is not the hypotenuse.

Predict: If you switch θ to the other acute angle, which side names will swap? Test it.

Explain: The hypotenuse stays fixed; opposite and adjacent swap. Turn or resize the triangle and these definitions still work. With the angle fixed, doubling every length leaves each ratio unchanged.

2 · Match the two sides to a ratio

SOH-CAH-TOA is a memory aid. Read each group as a function followed by a fraction: Sine = Opposite ÷ Hypotenuse, and so on.

SOH

sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse

CAH

cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse

TOA

tan θ = opposite / adjacent

Choose by sides, not by guessing. Mark the known side and the wanted side. O + H means sine; A + H means cosine; O + A means tangent. Ignore the unused side.

3 · Find a side or an angle

Use your calculator in degree mode (DEG). Write the ratio, substitute, rearrange, then calculate. Keep extra digits until the final answer.

Missing numerator: multiply

θ = 30°, hypotenuse = 12 cm. Find opposite.

sin 30° = x / 12
x = 12 × sin 30° = 6 cm

Missing denominator: divide

θ = 60°, adjacent = 5 cm. Find hypotenuse.

cos 60° = 5 / h
h × cos 60° = 5
h = 5 / cos 60° = 10 cm

Missing angle: use inverse trig

Opposite = 3 cm, adjacent = 4 cm. Find θ.

tan θ = 3 / 4
θ = tan⁻¹(3 / 4) ≈ 36.9°

The inverse function asks “which angle has this ratio?” On a calculator, use SHIFT/2nd + tan. Here tan⁻¹ means inverse tangent, not 1 / tan.

Sense check: The angle is between 0° and 90°. The hypotenuse must be longer than either leg. Sine and cosine are between 0 and 1; tangent can exceed 1.

4 · Choose → solve → explain

Work through six challenges: ratios, missing sides, then missing angles. Sketch each right triangle. Lengths use the same unit. Round calculated answers to one decimal place.

5 · Exit ticket

A ramp rises 1.2 m over a horizontal distance of 4 m. Which ratio finds its angle to the ground? Write the equation before using a calculator.

Compare your reasoning

The rise is opposite and the horizontal run is adjacent. TOA: tan θ = 1.2 / 4. θ = tan⁻¹(0.3) ≈ 16.7°. The ramp itself is the hypotenuse; the horizontal distance is not.
