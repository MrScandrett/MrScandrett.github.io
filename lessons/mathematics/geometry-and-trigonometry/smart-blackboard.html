About AI Smart Blackboards (Tongchuang & iFlytek)

In modern Chinese smart classrooms, AI blackboards (such as iFlytek's Tongchuang platform) replace static chalk with dynamic intelligence. When an instructor sketches a rough triangle or draws a 3D prism, computer vision neural networks classify the stroke sequence, snap it into clean Euclidean geometry, and immediately compute interior angles, side lengths, and area.

More importantly, the software introduces bidirectional construction and deconstruction: teachers don't just show static 3D solids; they smoothly unfold 3D polyhedra into flat 2D nets to reveal where surface area formulas come from, or slice cones and spheres with interactive cutting planes to bring cross-sections and Cavalieri's principle to life.

Real-Time Air Chalk: Computer Vision & Spatial Gestures

Clicking 📹 Air Chalk activates your webcam and launches client-side 3D hand tracking powered by Google MediaPipe. The vision model calculates the 3D coordinates $(x, y, z)$ of 21 skeletal landmarks on your hand in real time:

  • Pinch Detection (Pen Down): The Euclidean distance between your Thumb Tip (Landmark 4) and Index Fingertip (Landmark 8) is continuously measured and normalized against your hand scale. Bringing your thumb and pointer finger together activates Chalk Down mode.
  • Spatial Movement & Smoothing: The midpoint of your pinch maps directly across the blackboard canvas in mirrored selfie space. An adaptive velocity filter removes natural hand tremors while preserving responsive stroke paths.
  • Shape Snapping on Release: When you open your fingers (lifting the pen), the blackboard's geometric classifier analyzes the stroke path, automatically snapping rough air circles and triangles into Euclidean geometry.
  • 3D Spatial Manipulation: When viewing 3D solids (Cubes, Cylinders, Cones, Spheres), pinching in the air lets you tumble and rotate the 3D model, while depth movement ($z$-axis) can smoothly unfold 2D nets or drive Cavalieri slicing planes!

How This Works With ClassroomOS Geometry Lessons

This Smart Blackboard serves as the interactive capstone connecting key geometry and trigonometry lessons across the curriculum:

  • Pythagoras & Triangles (MAT-02): Provides the fundamental right-triangle relationship $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ used in altitude deconstruction, cone slant height $L = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$, and spherical cross-section radii $r(y) = \sqrt{R^2 - y^2}$.
  • SOH-CAH-TOA Trigonometry (MAT-03): Supplies sine, cosine, and tangent ratios to solve for unknown angles $\theta$ and missing sides $x$ in decomposed triangles and conical sectors.
  • Pi & Circles (MAT-01): Explains circle circumference $C = 2\pi r$, area $A = \pi r^2$, and cylinder volume, directly proving why a cylinder net's rectangular width is $2\pi r$ and why a cone unrolls into a sector of arc length $2\pi r$.
  • Area & Perimeter Playground (MAT-13): Establishes the 2D polygon decomposition methods used here to calculate the base perimeter and area of irregular prisms.
  • The Unit Circle & Waves (MAT-04): Connects radian arc length $s = r\theta$ to the cone sector unrolling angle $\theta = 360^\circ \times (r / L)$.
  • Area Under a Curve & Accumulation (MAT-19): Demonstrates Cavalieri's principle and Riemann disc integration: slicing a sphere into horizontal discs of area $A(y) = \pi(R^2 - y^2)$ accumulating to $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3$.
  • Blueprint Drafting & Sheet Metal Nets (3D-07): Connects theoretical 2D nets to real-world CAD flat patterns, fold lines, and sheet-metal manufacturing.

5 Core Geometric Deconstructions

  1. Triangles with Unknowns: Dropping an altitude $h \perp c$ divides any scalene triangle into two adjacent right triangles $\Delta ACD$ and $\Delta BCD$. Using the Pythagorean theorem and trigonometry, students can isolate and solve for any unknown side $x$, angle $\theta$, or height $h$.
  2. Turning a Cone into Flat 2D Slices & Sector Net:
    • 2D Slicing: Horizontal planes reveal circular discs whose radius tapers linearly $r(y) = r(1 - y/h)$. Tilted cuts produce ellipses, parabolas, and hyperbolas.
    • 2D Sector Net: Slitting the lateral wall along slant height $L = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$ unfolds into a circular sector of radius $L$ with central angle $\theta = 360^\circ \times \frac{r}{L}$ and arc length $2\pi r$, plus the base circular disc of radius $r$.
  3. Spheres & Cavalieri Cross-Sections: A horizontal slice at height $y$ yields a disc of radius $r = \sqrt{R^2 - y^2}$. Archimedes proved the sphere's surface area equals exactly 4 great circles ($4\pi R^2$), matching the lateral surface of its circumscribing cylinder.
  4. Irregular Prisms: An irregular polygonal base with side lengths $s_1, \dots, s_n$ unfolds into its top and bottom base polygons plus a continuous rectangular accordion ribbon of total length $P = \sum s_i$ and height $h$, proving $SA = 2B + P \cdot h$.
  5. Conjoined Surface Areas (Composite Solids): When a cylinder and cone are conjoined (such as a rocket or silo), the touching circular face is hidden inside. The exposed surface area is $SA_{\text{total}} = SA_{\text{cyl}} + SA_{\text{cone}} - 2 \cdot A_{\text{joint}}$.
