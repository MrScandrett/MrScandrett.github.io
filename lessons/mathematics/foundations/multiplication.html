Multiplication: see the structure.

From equal groups to area and scaling, multiplication connects small facts to big ideas. Build a model, choose a strategy, and explain why it works.

Grades 3–8 · 45–60 minutes · Work at your own pace

1. See it2. Think it3. Write it4. Extend it5. Try it

01 / Equal groups and arrays

Count groups, not one item at a time

Four trays with six muffins each contain 4 × 6 = 24 muffins. The numbers being multiplied are factors; the answer is the product. For whole-number groups, multiplication is repeated addition: 6 + 6 + 6 + 6 = 24.

An array arranges items in rows and columns. A rectangle with 4 rows and 6 columns holds 24 squares. If each square is 1 cm², its area is 24 cm²: length × width.

Predict: if you turn a 4-by-6 array, will the total change? If you split each row into 2 and 4, what two smaller products appear?

Turning the rectangle shows the commutative property: a × b = b × a. Splitting it shows the distributive property: a × (b + c) = a × b + a × c. The pieces still cover exactly the same area.

Multiplication and division undo each other: 4 × 6 = 24 gives 24 ÷ 6 = 4 and 24 ÷ 4 = 6. Use a division fact to check a nonzero factor.

02 / Build facts from facts you know

Mental-math tricks with reasons

Estimate before you calculate: 19 × 6 is close to 20 × 6 = 120, and must be a little less. Learn a small set of facts, then use structure to find the others.

Double, then double again

× 2 doubles. × 4 doubles twice. × 8 doubles three times. For 7 × 8: 7 → 14 → 28 → 56. This is the associative property: regroup factors without changing their product.

Use 5s and 10s

× 5 is half of × 10: 18 × 5 = 180 ÷ 2 = 90. Multiplying a whole number by 10 shifts each digit to the next place: 23 becomes 230. With decimals, 2.3 × 10 = 23; “add a zero” is not a universal rule.

Use a nearby friendly number

× 9 = × 10 − one group: 7 × 9 = 70 − 7 = 63. For 19 × 6, use 20 × 6 − 6 = 114. For 21 × 6, use 20 × 6 + 6 = 126.

Split into place values

23 × 7 = (20 + 3) × 7 = 140 + 21 = 161. For 6 × 7, you could use 6 × 5 + 6 × 2 = 30 + 12 = 42. The best split depends on the facts you know.

Double one, halve the other

16 × 25 = 8 × 50 = 4 × 100 = 400. Multiplying one factor by 2 and dividing the other by 2 preserves the product. Doubling both factors makes the product four times as big.

Use 25 and 50

× 25 = × 100 then ÷ 4: 28 × 25 = 2800 ÷ 4 = 700. × 50 = × 100 then ÷ 2: 14 × 50 = 1400 ÷ 2 = 700. These work because 25 = 100/4 and 50 = 100/2.

Strategy explorer

The × 11 trick has a reason: 47 × 11 = 470 + 47 = 517. Adding 4 + 7 gives 11, so the tens column needs a carry. Simply putting “11” between the digits would be wrong. Use × 10 + the original number whenever a digit trick becomes confusing.

Avoid the trap: (20 + 3) × (10 + 4) needs four products, not just 20 × 10 and 3 × 4. Each part of one factor multiplies each part of the other.

03 / Place value makes the method work

Area → partial products → written multiplication

For 23 × 14, split 23 into 20 + 3 and 14 into 10 + 4. Each rectangle below is a partial product. Add them to find the whole area.

20 × 10 = 200Tens × tens
3 × 10 = 30Ones × tens
20 × 4 = 80Tens × ones
3 × 4 = 12Ones × ones

200 + 30 + 80 + 12 = 322

    Why shift the second row? In 23 × 14, the 1 in 14 means 10, so that row represents 23 × 10 = 230, not 23 × 1 = 23. A written zero records the empty ones place. Align columns by place value.
    

    04 / Multiplication is also scaling
    “Times” does not always mean bigger
    Equal groups explain whole-number multiplication. For fractions and decimals, think of scaling: a factor tells you how much of the original amount to take.
    Original: 8 units
    Both bars use the same scale; full track = 16 units.
    Fractions: part of a part
    ¾ × 8 = 6. For ½ × ¾, take half of three quarters: 3/8. Multiply numerators and denominators: (1 × 3)/(2 × 4) = 3/8. Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions first: 1½ × 2 = 3/2 × 2 = 3.
    Decimals: track the units
    0.4 × 0.3 = 0.12: four tenths times three tenths is twelve hundredths. For 2.4 × 1.3, calculate 24 × 13 = 312, then divide by 100 because each factor was multiplied by 10: 3.12. Count decimal places, and check with an estimate.
    Zero, one, and signs
    a × 0 = 0: zero groups contribute nothing. a × 1 = a: one copy stays the same. A positive quantity multiplied by a positive factor below 1 becomes smaller; above 1, bigger.
    Different signs give a negative product; the same signs give a positive product. −3 × 4 = −12; −3 × −4 = 12. To keep distribution true, 0 = −3 × (4 + −4) = −12 + (−3 × −4), so the missing product must be 12.
    Units and meaning
    4 boxes × 6 pencils per box = 24 pencils. 4 cm × 6 cm = 24 cm². Multiplication also counts combinations: 3 shirts with 2 pairs of trousers give 3 × 2 = 6 outfits.
    Multiplication and division share a priority: work left to right unless grouping says otherwise. 12 ÷ 3 × 2 = 8.
    

    05 / Build your multiplication toolkit
    Practice with a plan
    Estimate, choose a strategy, calculate, and check. Explain your route to a partner. Accuracy and reasoning come before speed.
    0 solved independently in this session.
    Exit ticket
    1. Find 18 × 25 in two different ways. Explain why each works.
    2. Explain why the second row in 23 × 14 is 230.
    3. Predict whether 12 × ¾ is greater or less than 12, then calculate it.
    Check the exit ticket
    1. 450: 18 × 100 ÷ 4, or 9 × 50, or 20 × 25 − 2 × 25. 2. The 1 in 14 represents one ten, so multiply 23 by 10. 3. Less: taking three quarters of 12 gives 9.
