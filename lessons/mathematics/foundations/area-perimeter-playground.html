Area & Perimeter Playground

Area = 2D surface inside Perimeter = 1D boundary around Polyominoes, Parallelograms & Triangles

Area and perimeter measure two fundamentally different things about a shape: how many square units cover its interior versus how many linear units wrap around its outer boundary. Build shapes on a polyomino grid to see which edges count toward perimeter, then shear parallelograms and unfold twin triangles to see why their area formulas work.

Learning Target

I can find the area and perimeter of grid shapes, rectangles, parallelograms, and triangles, and explain why shapes with the same perimeter can enclose different areas.

By the end of the lesson, students will distinguish interior unit squares from exterior boundary segments, prove why shared interior edges reduce perimeter, and use geometric rearrangement to derive A = b × h for parallelograms and A = ½ × b × h for triangles.

Area vs. Perimeter & Core Formulas

Area measures 2D surface coverage in square units (units2). Perimeter measures 1D boundary distance in linear units.

Rectangle AreaA = ℓ × w
Count the unit squares arranged in ℓ columns and w rows.
Outer PerimeterP = 2(ℓ + w)
Sum all outer boundary edges. Shared inner edges do not count!
ParallelogramA = b × h
Use perpendicular height h (not the slanted side c) times base b.
TriangleA = ½ × b × h
Every triangle is exactly half of a b × h parallelogram.

Worked Example

Compare a 4 × 3 rectangle with a parallelogram of base b = 6, perpendicular height h = 4, and slanted side c = 5.

Rectangle Area = 4 × 3 = 12 sq units
4 columns of 3 unit squares fit inside the rectangle.
Rectangle Perimeter = 2 × (4 + 3) = 14 units
Walking around the outside boundary covers 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 14 linear units.
Parallelogram Area = 6 × 4 = 24 sq units
Multiply base (6) by perpendicular height (4). Ignore the slanted side (5) when finding area!
Parallelogram Perimeter = 2 × (6 + 5) = 22 units
Perimeter follows the actual outside fence: two bases of 6 plus two slanted sides of 5.

Formulas
Rectangle A = ℓ × w (sq units) · P = 2(ℓ + w) (linear units).
Parallelogram A = b × h using perpendicular height h.
Triangle A = ½ b × h (half of its twin parallelogram).
Common Traps
Same perimeter ≠ same area Compact shapes hide shared edges inside; thin strips expose edges outside.
Slant height trap Use perpendicular height h for area, and slanted side c only for perimeter.
