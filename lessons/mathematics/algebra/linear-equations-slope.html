Linear Equations & Slope

Slope as rise over run Slope-intercept form (y = mx + b) Parallel & perpendicular lines Real-world rate of change

Every non-vertical straight line has a constant steepness and a starting height on the vertical axis. In this multi-lab lesson, you will drag points to visualize the rise / run right triangle, test parallel and perpendicular slopes, and connect y = mx + b to real-world rates of change.

Learning Target

I can find the slope and equation of a line from two points, a graph, or a table, and explain what slope and y-intercept mean in context.

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to compute slope as m = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁), locate the y-intercept (0, b), identify parallel and perpendicular slopes, and model constant rates of change in y = mx + b form.

Slope-Intercept Form & The Four Types of Slope

Any non-vertical line can be written as y = mx + b, where m is the slope (constant rate of change) and b is the y-intercept (the value of y when x = 0).

Slope (m = Δy / Δx)How steep the line is. Between any two points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂), slope is rise / run = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁).
Y-intercept (0, b)Where the line crosses the vertical y-axis. It represents the initial or starting value when x = 0.
Positive (m > 0)Line rises from left to right. As x increases, y increases.
Negative (m < 0)Line falls from left to right. As x increases, y decreases.
Zero (m = 0)Horizontal line (y = b). Rise is 0 no matter how far you run.
Undefined (Δx = 0)Vertical line (x = c). Run is 0, and division by zero is undefined.

Worked Example

Find the equation of the line passing through (1, 2) and (4, 8).

m = (8 - 2) / (4 - 1)
Slope is change in y (Δy) divided by change in x (Δx) between the two points.
m = 6 / 3 = 2
The line rises 2 units for every 1 unit it runs to the right.
2 = 2(1) + b
Substitute one point (1, 2) and the slope m = 2 into y = mx + b to solve for b.
b = 0
Subtract 2 from both sides: 2 - 2 = b, so the line crosses the y-axis at the origin (0, 0).
y = 2x
Write the final equation in slope-intercept form.

Slope-Intercept Form
y = mx + b m is the slope (Δy / Δx), and b is the y-intercept at (0, b).
Parallel vs. Perpendicular Parallel lines share the same slope (m₁ = m₂). Perpendicular slopes multiply to -1 (m₂ = -1/m₁).
Common Trap
Negative slope The line falls left to right; when you run right (+Δx), the rise is negative (-Δy).
Zero vs. Undefined Horizontal lines have slope 0 (y = b). Vertical lines have undefined slope (x = c).
