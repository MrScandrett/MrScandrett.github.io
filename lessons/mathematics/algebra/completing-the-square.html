Why half? The bx area is split into two equal strips. Their shared width is b/2, so the corner has area (b/2)².
Al-Jabr · 820 CE Al-Khwarizmi's Geometric Cut-and-Move Proof
1. Start with x² and bx An x × x square next to a 6 × x rectangle of area 6x.
2. Slice the bx rectangle lengthwise Cut the 6x rectangle into two equal 3 × x strips.
3. Form the L-shaped gnomon Move one 3x strip to the bottom edge of x², leaving an empty 3 × 3 corner notch.
4. Fill the (b/2)² corner square Add the 3 × 3 = 9 corner square to complete (x + 3)²!

How Completing the Square Derives the Quadratic Formula x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a
Step 1 · General Quadratic ax² + bx + c = 0 Start with any quadratic equation where a ≠ 0.
Step 2 · Normalize a = 1 & Move Constant x² + (b/a)x = −c/a Divide every term by a and subtract c/a from both sides.
Step 3 · Add Missing Corner (b / 2a)² x² + (b/a)x + b²/(4a²) = b²/(4a²) − c/a Half of b/a is b/(2a); squaring gives b²/(4a²).
Step 4 · Factor & Common Denominator (x + b/(2a))² = (b² − 4ac) / (4a²) Combine the right side over 4a² to reveal the discriminant b² − 4ac.
Step 5 · Take Square Root (±) x + b/(2a) = ±√(b² − 4ac) / (2a) Take both positive and negative square roots of each side.
Step 6 · Isolate x (Quadratic Formula) x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / (2a) Subtract b/(2a). Every quadratic formula application is completing the square in disguise!
