What did the machine really change?

A number needs a unit.

10 kg tells you a mass. 10 L tells you a volume. 10 in tells you a length. Those are different kinds of measurement, even though the number is the same.

In imagination mode, the machine invents a new object. In real conversion mode, it keeps the same mass and changes only its description: 10 kg = 10,000 g. The weight must not shrink!

To connect mass and volume, you need another fact: density. About 10 kg of water occupies 10 L near room temperature; 10 kg of iron occupies much less space. That is a material relationship, not a universal kg-to-L conversion.

Factory fieldwork

  1. Make 10 V, 10 L, and 10 in. What kind of measurement does each describe?
  2. Compare 10 mm, 10 cm, and 10 in. Predict which is longest before transforming.
  3. Switch to real conversion. Try g and lb. Does the mass or the number change?
  4. Find two units that describe electricity but measure different quantities.

    Why Numbers Need Units "10" has no physical reality until a unit gives it dimension. 10 kg is mass; 10 litres is volume; 10 inches is spatial length.
    The Seven SI Base Units All physical measurements derive from 7 fundamentals: metre (length), kilogram (mass), second (time), ampere (electric current), kelvin (temperature), mole (amount), candela (luminous intensity).
    Dimensional Analysis Rule You can multiply and divide quantities with different units (e.g. \(\text{mass} / \text{volume} = \text{density}\)), but you can NEVER add or subtract them (\(10\text{ kg} + 10\text{ m}\) is undefined).
    Material Equivalence 10 kg of water occupies exactly ~10 L near 4°C, but 10 kg of gold occupies only 0.52 L because gold is 19.3× denser than water.
