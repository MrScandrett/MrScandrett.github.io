What did the machine really change?

A number needs a unit.

10 kg tells you a mass. 10 L tells you a volume. 10 in tells you a length. Those are different kinds of measurement, even though the number is the same.

In imagination mode, the machine invents a new object. In real conversion mode, it keeps the same mass and changes only its description: 10 kg = 10,000 g. The weight must not shrink!

To connect mass and volume, you need another fact: density. About 10 kg of water occupies 10 L near room temperature; 10 kg of iron occupies much less space. That is a material relationship, not a universal kg-to-L conversion. Mass and weight are linked the same way: by the gravity where you stand.

The machine shows its working as a factor-label chain: 10 kg × (1,000 g / 1 kg) = 10,000 g. Each fraction equals 1, so it changes the number but not the amount, and every unit on a top cancels the same unit on a bottom.

Factory fieldwork

  1. Make 10 V, 10 L, and 10 in. What kind of measurement does each describe?
  2. Flip the breaker to Same mass. Try g and lb. Does the balance tip? Which units cancel in the chain?
  3. Predict before you transform. If you are off by 1,000×, which prefix tripped you up?
  4. Take the gold, water and cork crates off the shelf. Which 10 kg cube is biggest, and why?
  5. Weigh 10 kg on the Moon and on Jupiter. The weight looks the same size. What changed?

    Why Numbers Need Units "10" has no physical reality until a unit gives it dimension. 10 kg is mass; 10 litres is volume; 10 inches is spatial length.
    The Seven SI Base Units All physical measurements derive from 7 fundamentals: metre (length), kilogram (mass), second (time), ampere (electric current), kelvin (temperature), mole (amount), candela (luminous intensity).
    Dimensional Analysis Rule You can multiply and divide quantities with different units (e.g. mass / volume = density), but you can NEVER add or subtract them (10 kg + 10 m is undefined).
    Material Equivalence 10 kg of water occupies exactly ~10 L near 4°C, but 10 kg of gold occupies only 0.52 L because gold is 19.3× denser than water.
