Mean, Median & Mode

Mean = the balance point (x̄) Median = the middle position Mode = the most frequent value

Mean, median, and mode all try to describe a "typical" value in a dataset, but they respond very differently to skewness and outliers. In this lesson, you will drag points on a number-line balance beam, test how odd and even counts change the median, and explore when a headline "average" is telling the truth — or hiding an outlier.

Learning Target

I can calculate the mean, median, and mode of a dataset, model the mean as a physical balance point, and justify which measure of center best represents a real-world distribution.

By the end of the lesson, you will be able to compute all three measures for odd- and even-sized datasets and explain why the median is resistant to extreme outliers while the mean shifts toward them.

Three Measures of Center

Each measure defines "center" differently, and each answers a different question about the same numbers.

Mean (x̄) Balance Point
Mean = (x₁ + x₂ + … + xₙ) / n
Add every value and divide by the count n. Physical fulcrum where signed deviations cancel out: Σ(xi − x̄) = 0. Sensitive to outliers.
Median Middle Rank
Odd n: middle · Even n: avg of 2 middle
Sort from least to greatest and take the middle value (or average the two middle values when n is even). Resistant to outliers.
Mode Most Common
Value(s) with highest frequency
The value that occurs most often. A dataset can have no mode, one mode (unimodal), or multiple modes (bimodal/multimodal).

Worked Example

Find the mean, median, and mode of the 5-value dataset 4, 7, 7, 9, 3 — and see what happens if a 6th value (12) joins.

Step 1 · Sort first: 3, 4, 7, 7, 9
Sorting immediately reveals repeated values and prevents taking the middle of an unsorted list!
Mean = (3 + 4 + 7 + 7 + 9) / 5 = 30 / 5 = 6
Sum all 5 values (30) and divide by n = 5 to get the balance point x̄ = 6.
Median (odd n = 5) = 7
With 5 sorted values, the 3rd value (3, 4, [7], 7, 9) sits dead center.
Mode = 7
The number 7 appears twice; every other number appears once.
Even-Count Check (n = 6): 3, 4, [7, 7], 9, 12
When n is even, average the two middle numbers: (7 + 7) / 2 = 7. (For 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12, Median = (6 + 8) / 2 = 7).

Definitions
Mean (x̄) sum of values divided by the count n. Acts as the center-of-mass balance fulcrum.
Median the middle value in sorted order (or the mean of the two middle values when n is even).
Mode the most frequent value(s).
Common Traps
Forgetting to Sort Taking the middle number of an unsorted list gives a random position, not the median!
Outlier Pull Outliers pull the mean toward the long tail, which is why household income and home-price reports use the median.
