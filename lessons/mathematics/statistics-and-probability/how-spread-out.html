How Spread Out?

Range Quartiles & IQR Box plots Standard deviation The bell curve

Two basketball players both average 10 points a game. One scores 10 every night. The other scores 0 one night and 20 the next. The average cannot tell them apart. Spread can. Drag data points around, test consistency against a target score, and explore the 68–95–99.7 rule.

Lab 3 · The Bell Curve & the 68–95–99.7 Rule

Many measurements pile up in a bell shape: heights, test scores, measurement errors, and balls in a pachinko board. About 68% of values fall within 1σ of the mean, 95% within 2σ, and 99.7% within 3σ. Learning Targets & How Each Measure Works

  • Range = largest − smallest. Simple, but one wild value ruins it.
  • Q1, median, Q3 cut the sorted data into four equal-sized groups. IQR = Q3 − Q1, the spread of the middle half.
  • Outlier fences (1.5 × IQR rule): values below Q1 − 1.5 × IQR or above Q3 + 1.5 × IQR are flagged as outliers.
  • Standard deviation (σ): find each value's distance from the mean, square them, average the squares (variance σ²), then take the square root. It is the typical distance from the mean.
  • z-score: z = (x − μ) ÷ σ tells how many standard deviations a value lies above or below the mean.

Prerequisites: Mean, Median & Mode. Next: Galton Board & Pachinko Lab, where a bell curve emerges from falling balls.

Practice

Answers within 0.05 count.

1. Data: 3, 5, 8, 10, 15. What is the range?

2. Data: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7. The median is 4. Q1 is the median of 1, 2, 3 and Q3 is the median of 5, 6, 7. What is the IQR?

3. Data: 2, 4, 6, 8 (mean 5). What is the population standard deviation, to two decimal places?

4. Scores have μ = 70 and σ = 10. What is the top of the middle 68%?

5. About what percent of a bell-curve data set lies within 2 standard deviations of the mean?

6. z-score of 85 when μ = 70 and σ = 10?

7. Test B has μ = 20 and σ = 4. What is the z-score of a 28? (The bigger z is the more impressive result.)

8. Q1 = 8 and Q3 = 12. Values above what number count as outliers? (Upper fence = Q3 + 1.5 × IQR.)

Exit Ticket

  • Two data sets have the same mean and median. How could one still be much more spread out than the other?
  • A single extreme value is added. Which changes more: the IQR or the standard deviation, and why?
  • Describe a real situation where consistency (small σ) matters more than the average.
