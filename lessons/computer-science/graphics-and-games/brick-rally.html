      Racing

      Build it, scan it, race it

      Pick a car or build one brick by brick, or photograph a real brick car and let the game rebuild it. Then race the bots, or press Host a race and everyone on the same WiFi sees your race appear in their list. Arrow keys or WASD drive, Space brakes, R puts you back on the road. Phones get on-screen pedals, and a game controller works too.

      How the scanner works: space carving

      Find the car

      The scanner reads the colour around the photo's edge as "background", measures how different every pixel is from it, and picks the cut-off automatically (Otsu's method). The biggest blob that's left is the car.

      Each photo is a stencil

      The side photo says which cells, looking from the side, have car in them. So do the front and top photos. A brick survives only if all three stencils cover it. Computer vision calls this shape the visual hull.

      Colour from whoever sees it

      Every pixel colour is matched to the nearest real brick colour. Roof bricks take their colour from the top photo, the nose from the front, the rest from the side.

      What it can't see

      A hollow no photo looks into (a cockpit seen only from the side) comes out solid. That's why the game drops you into Build afterwards: the scanner gets the shape, you fix the details.

      How phones on the same WiFi find each other

      No server of our own

      This site is just files. So the game borrows three free public things: a STUN server, a few public MQTT "message boards", and WebRTC, which is built into every browser.

      "Which WiFi am I on?"

      STUN tells a browser what address the internet sees it coming from. Every device behind one WiFi router shares that address (that's NAT). The game hashes it into a channel name, so the address itself is never posted, and listens there for races.

      Hosting

      A host posts a small "race here, code BRKZ" note to that channel every two seconds. Anyone else on the WiFi sees it within a moment. The 4-letter code works from anywhere, even on a different network.

      Then go direct

      Once you're in, your browser opens a WebRTC data channel straight to the host's. Car positions fly 20 times a second without leaving the building. If the network blocks that, the game quietly carries on through the message board.

      Driver names and car designs travel through public relays, so use a nickname, never a full name. Nothing is saved anywhere except your own browser.

      Challenges

      1. Read the stats. Build the fastest car you can. What did you give up? Why does a tall car lose grip?
      2. Beat the hull. Build a real car whose scan comes out wrong. Which photo would you add to fix it?
      3. Network detective. Host a race and watch the lobby: does each player say "direct" or "via relay"? Try it on a phone hotspot. What changed?
      4. Lag lab. The game draws other cars where they probably are now, guessing from their last speed. When does that guess look wrong?
