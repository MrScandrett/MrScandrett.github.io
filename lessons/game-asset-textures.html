Lesson 01 · 60 minutes · Surface design

Make a surface tell the truth.

A texture is not decoration pasted onto a model. It is evidence: color, roughness, tiny height changes, wear, and scale tell the player whether a surface is wood, steel, cloth, stone, or plastic.

01 / READ

Material observation

Separate color from physical behavior.

BASE COLOR

What color is it?

Record broad color variation without painted lighting or deep shadows.

ROUGHNESS

How blurry is reflection?

White is usually rough; black is usually smooth. Fingerprints and wear create variation.

NORMAL

Which tiny bumps bend light?

Use surface relief smaller than the silhouette. Large shape changes belong in geometry.

CC0-first source rule: Photograph your own surface, paint it yourself, or use a verified CC0/public-domain texture. Do not use generated imagery or a download whose creator and license cannot be identified. Save the asset-page URL and license beside every downloaded source.
FIELD / NOTES

Patterns from working artists

Build in passes, then give every mark a reason.

01 · CORE

Begin clean

Establish color, reflectivity, and scale before adding age. Arkane’s Deathloop team keeps the core, alteration, wear, and dirt in separate passes.

02 · HIERARCHY

Read big, medium, small

Large forms identify the material at a distance, medium variation breaks repetition, and small detail rewards a close look.

03 · SYSTEM + HAND

Generate, then correct

Tileable layers do the repeatable work. Masks and hand painting remove generic marks and place unique evidence where the story needs it.

04 · CAUSE

Let use place the wear

Hands polish contact zones, impacts expose edges, and moisture settles low. Roughness often tells this story better than extra color noise.

The shared technique: observe first, author channels separately, vary detail by scale, and finish with restrained, cause-based wear—not an all-over grunge filter.

Field sources: Arkane’s layered pipeline · Bell Island’s big–medium–small rule · procedural + hand-painted workflows

02 / TILE

No visible borders

Build a square that can repeat forever.

  1. Start at 1024 × 1024.This is large enough to learn on without wasting memory. Crop a front-facing patch with even light.
  2. Offset by half the width and height.The old outer edges now meet in the center, where the seam is easy to see.
  3. Heal across the cross-shaped seam.Clone or paint with a soft, irregular brush. Do not erase every distinctive mark.
  4. Preview a 3 × 3 repeat.Look for obvious grids, repeated knots, brightness bands, and mismatched edges.
  5. Export losslessly.Use PNG for authored maps. Name files consistently: wall_plaster_basecolor.png, wall_plaster_roughness.png.

03 / TEST

Blender material

Test the material on a meter-sized plane.

  1. Add a plane and set a known size.Use N → Item → Dimensions. Apply scale with Ctrl+A → Scale.
  2. Create a Principled BSDF material.Connect base color as sRGB; set roughness and normal data to Non-Color.
  3. Route the normal map correctly.Image Texture → Normal Map node → Principled BSDF Normal input.
  4. View under a grazing light.Rotate a large Area Light until highlights reveal roughness and normal detail.
  5. Change mapping scale.Make bricks, boards, or weave match a believable real-world size.

TOOLS

Free, fast, and professional apps

Choose a texture tool by the job.

FREEPRO PIPELINE

Blender

Paint directly on models, build procedural shaders, bake maps, unwrap UVs, and test the exact material on the final mesh. Best no-cost choice when one app must cover the full pipeline.

Open Blender’s official site ↗
FREEUNDER 30 MIN

Material Maker

A focused, open-source node editor for procedural PBR materials. Its community library and Godot-ready exports make it an unusually fast way to learn tiling materials.

Try Material Maker ↗
PAIDPRO TEXTURING

Substance 3D Painter

A specialist production tool for layer-based 3D painting, masks, smart materials, baking, and engine export. Choose it when many detailed assets need a consistent professional texturing workflow.

Explore Painter ↗
PAIDFAST CAPTURE

Substance 3D Sampler

Turns photographs and scans into editable material starting points. It is the paid fast lane when the goal is to capture a real wall, fabric, wood, or floor rather than invent one from zero.

Explore Sampler ↗
