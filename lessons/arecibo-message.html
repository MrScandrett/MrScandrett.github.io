The Arecibo Message

In 1974, humanity aimed the world's largest radio telescope at a star cluster 25,000 light-years away and broadcast a binary message. Can you decode what we said?

Binary Primer

How it works

The message contains only 1679 bits — each either a 1 (transmitted pulse) or a 0 (silence). To read it, you must arrange those bits into a rectangle. Only one arrangement makes an image: 23 columns × 73 rows.

Why Prime Numbers?

1679 = 23 × 73. Because both are prime, there are only two ways to arrange the bits into a rectangle: 23 × 73 or 73 × 23 Any intelligent receiver would try both. Only 73 rows × 23 columns reveals a clear image, proving the signal is intentional.

The Story

  • 📡Broadcast on November 16, 1974 to celebrate the Arecibo Observatory upgrade in Puerto Rico.
  • 👨‍🔬Designed by Frank Drake and Carl Sagan with Cornell University scientists.
  • 🌌Aimed at Messier 13, a star cluster 25,000 light-years away containing ~300,000 stars.
  • 💬The message was largely symbolic—a demonstration of human technological capability. Arecibo itself was decommissioned in 2020.
