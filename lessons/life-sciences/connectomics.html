Connectomics

Connectomics is the study of the connectome: the comprehensive map of neural connections in the brain (its "wiring diagram").

The Fruit Fly Milestone

Mapping a brain is incredibly difficult because neurons are tiny and densely packed. In 2024, the FlyWire project achieved a historic milestone: they mapped the entire adult male fruit fly brain, detailing over 140,000 neurons and 50 million synapses.

Wiring Creates Behavior

Why map these connections? Because how neurons wire to each other directly determines how an organism processes information and behaves. By tracing the paths from sensory inputs (like eyes) to motor outputs (like wings), we can understand the physical basis of behavior.

Simulation: The Architecture of Behavior

In the 1980s, Valentino Braitenberg proposed simple hypothetical vehicles to show how complex behaviors emerge from very simple neural wiring. In this simulation, the "fly" has two sensors (detecting the food source) and two motors (driving the left and right wheels).

Try changing the neural wiring below and observe how the fly's behavior changes entirely!
