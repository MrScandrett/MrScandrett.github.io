Evidence lab · 20–30 min

Animalia · Body-plan evidence

Different bodies, shared ancestry

Compare six selected animal phyla using symmetry, support, and movement. There are many other animal phyla, including Annelida (segmented worms) and Nematoda (roundworms).

Big idea: A phylum is a broad group based on evolutionary relationships and body-plan evidence. Invertebrates span many phyla; some chordates are also invertebrates.

Step 1 · Predict

Do two radial animals share a close ancestor?

A sea star and a jellyfish both look radial as adults. Does this alone establish that they are each other’s closest relatives? Record a prediction, then compare their structures.

Research the six phyla

Step 3 · Test your explanation

Classify a mystery animal

An unknown marine animal has bilateral larvae, a five-part adult body, and tube feet operated by a water vascular system. Which selected phylum best fits? Which evidence is most useful?

Compare with an explanation

Echinodermata fits. Tube feet and a water vascular system are stronger evidence than adult symmetry alone. Jellyfish have stinging cells and a different body plan. A single shared appearance does not establish closest ancestry.

Reflect

Revise your prediction. Explain why “no backbone” does not mean “no internal skeleton,” using a sea star as evidence. Body plans support classification; DNA and developmental evidence also help reconstruct ancestry.

Sources and model limits

This is a selected comparison, not a complete evolutionary tree or a species identification key. Traits vary within phyla and across life stages.
