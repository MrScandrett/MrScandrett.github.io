Ocean Science Essentials

🌈 Where Do the Colors Go?

Water attenuates wavelengths at different rates: red is absorbed first, while blue-green travels farthest in clear ocean water. There is no single cutoff depth—the result changes with plankton, sediment, weather, and viewing conditions. Photosynthesis is generally limited to the upper ~200 m; below 1,000 m, sunlight is effectively absent.

💥 Pressure & Adaptations

In seawater, absolute pressure rises by about 1 atmosphere every 10.1 m. Challenger Deep is near 1,100 atmospheres. Many deep-sea animals reduce gas spaces, use pressure-tolerant proteins and membranes, and accumulate molecules such as TMAO that help proteins function under pressure.

✨ Why Do They Glow?

Bioluminescence is widespread in the open ocean. Anglerfish use luminous bacteria in a lure, hatchetfish counterilluminate their silhouette, and some squid flash or release glowing material. Different organisms use different chemistries, commonly involving a light-emitting molecule called luciferin.

🛶 Famous Dives

In 1960, Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh rode the bathyscaphe Trieste to the Challenger Deep — the first humans to reach the ocean's deepest point. Filmmaker James Cameron repeated the feat solo in 2012. In 2019, Victor Vescovo's Five Deeps Expedition became the first to reach the deepest point of all five oceans.
