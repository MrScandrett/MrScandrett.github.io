Hedy Lamarr

Frequency Hopping Spread Spectrum Hollywood Star Self-Taught Inventor

Hedy Lamarr (1914–2000) was an actor and a self-taught inventor. With musician George Antheil, she designed a way for a radio sender and receiver to change channels together. Their work is an early milestone in the history of wireless communication. How can a shared pattern help a message get through?

Start with a Shared Pattern

Big question: How can we use creativity and teamwork to help people communicate?

Teacher plan · 30–40 minutes: Wonder and predict (5 min), meet the inventors (5 min), test channels with cards or the lab (10–15 min), explain the evidence and reflect (10 min). Materials: paper, pencil, and two sets of five numbered cards per group. No account or radio equipment needed.

Learning goal: Explain why a sender and receiver must change radio channels together, and describe how Lamarr and Antheil combined different skills.

  • K–2, together: Read the introduction aloud. Use the channel-card activity below, count successful messages, and explain the pattern with words or pointing.
  • Grades 3–5: Read the biography, compare the two diagrams, and try practice questions 1, 2, 4, and 7.
  • Grades 6–8: Add the historical context and technical notes. Calculate the fraction of messages received and explain why timing matters.
  • Grades 9–12: Complete all questions, evaluate the patent as primary evidence, and design a fair comparison where hopping does not outperform a fixed channel.

Words to know: A frequency describes how quickly a radio wave repeats. A radio channel uses a particular frequency range. Interference is an unwanted signal that makes a message harder to receive. Synchronized means coordinated in time.

Who Was Hedy Lamarr?

Hedy Lamarr was born Hedwig Eva Maria Kiesler in Vienna, Austria, in 1914. She worked as an actor in Europe and later in Hollywood, using the stage name Hedy Lamarr. She also explored engineering ideas outside her film career.

Lamarr teamed up with composer George Antheil to design a radio system that changed frequencies in a coordinated pattern. Antheil's experience with player pianos helped them think about timing: matching punched paper rolls could tell the sender and receiver when to switch channels.

They received U.S. Patent 2,292,387 in 1942. A patent records an invention and names its inventors; this one names Hedy Kiesler Markey (Lamarr's legal name at the time) and George Antheil. A patent alone does not show that a design was built or widely used.

Historical context for older students: why 1942?
During World War II, they proposed the system to reduce deliberate interference, called jamming, in radio guidance for naval torpedoes, which are underwater weapons. The Navy did not adopt their design during the war. Here we study the communication idea using channel cards and everyday interference.

What Frequency Hopping Actually Does

Imagine two devices sending a message across a classroom. If they always use channel 3, another signal on channel 3 may interfere with the message. Frequency hopping means changing channels in a shared pattern. The sender and receiver must use the same channel at the same time.

Moving between channels can reduce the time spent on one noisy channel. It cannot remove all interference or guarantee that every message arrives. It also does not make a message secret by itself; modern devices use separate security methods.

Try It: Channel Cards

Predict: Will changing channels help if the receiver does not change with the sender? Explain your idea before testing.

  1. Make two sets of cards numbered 1–5. A sender and a receiver each have one set. A third student observes and counts.
  2. Agree that channel 3 is noisy. First, both partners hold up 3 for eight turns. Count how many turns avoid channel 3.
  3. Now both follow 3, 5, 2, 4, 1, 3, 5, 2, one card each time the teacher says “next.” Count the turns on clear channels. Predict first, then check: did six of eight get through?
  4. Try again with the receiver one turn behind. Compare the cards: why is a shared pattern alone insufficient without shared timing?

Model limit: Cards stand for radio channels; no radio equipment is needed. We assume noise blocks channel 3 completely and all other channels are clear. Real signals, noise, timing, and recovery of missing data are more complicated.

Technical note: what does “spread spectrum” mean?
Spread-spectrum methods spread a signal's use of radio frequencies over a wider range. Frequency hopping does this by changing channels over time. Other methods spread the signal differently; spread spectrum does not always mean hopping.
Technical note: how does this relate to Bluetooth?
Bluetooth uses frequency hopping with digital electronics. It shares the idea of coordinated channel changes with Lamarr and Antheil's design, rather than using their paper-roll mechanism. Modern wireless devices reflect the work of many inventors and engineers.

Can Your Message Get Through?

Send eight message pieces, one turn at a time. Compare a fixed channel with the shared pattern 3 → 5 → 2 → 4 → 1 → 3 → 5 → 2. Keep the noise and timing settings the same for a fair comparison.

Ready. Predict how many of eight pieces will arrive, then send the first piece.

TurnSender channelReceiver channelResult

Record: Strategy, noisy channel, timing, and pieces received out of eight. Change one setting, predict again, and repeat. Changing a setting starts a new trial.

Investigate: Try noise on channel 1. Can a fixed channel do better? Try no interference with the receiver one turn behind. Does hopping still work?

Model limits: Each turn is one new message piece; missed pieces are not resent. Noise blocks one channel completely. The late receiver starts at the last channel in the pattern. This is a channel-and-timing model, not a working radio or a demonstration of encryption.

Faith & Purpose: Create to Serve

In a Christian classroom, we study the natural world as God's creation and practice using our abilities to love our neighbors. Engineering gives us opportunities to listen carefully, solve useful problems, and work with people whose gifts differ from our own.

  • Honor each person · Genesis 1:27: People bear God's image. How can we recognize a classmate's ideas and abilities without judging by appearance, popularity, or background?
  • Use gifts to serve · 1 Peter 4:10: Lamarr and Antheil brought different skills to one problem. What can you contribute to a team, and whose help could you welcome?
  • Practice honesty · Ephesians 4:25: Report missed messages as carefully as successful ones. Why would hiding a failed trial make a design less trustworthy?

Design for a neighbor: Imagine a classroom alert that helps someone notice an important announcement. Who needs it? Would sound, light, or vibration help them? Sketch a design, ask for feedback, and explain how you would test it respectfully.

Lamarr's Key Contributions

📡
Frequency-Hopping Patent (1942)

With George Antheil, patented a "Secret Communication System" using synchronized frequency changes to resist radio jamming.

📶
An Early Wireless Milestone

Their patent is an early example of coordinated frequency hopping. Bluetooth provides a modern example of the same general technique.

🎥
Decades-Long Hollywood Career

Worked as a professional actor while also exploring technical problems. Her interests crossed the arts and engineering.

🔬
Self-Taught Engineering

Pursued invention as a hobby outside any formal STEM career or training, working problems out at home between film shoots.

🎵
Cross-Disciplinary Collaboration

Combined her radio ideas with George Antheil's experience coordinating mechanical music instruments.

🏆
Recognition Across Decades

Her invention was recognized during her lifetime in 1997 and after her death with a 2014 National Inventors Hall of Fame induction.

Lamarr's Legacy Today

Lamarr's story invites us to look at a person's work across more than one field. An acting career and an interest in engineering can exist together. Her collaboration with Antheil shows how musical timing and radio design can meet in a new idea.

Give both collaborators credit, and distinguish an early patent from a finished modern device. Bluetooth is a useful comparison because it uses frequency hopping. Wi-Fi and GPS use different radio methods; they should not be described as direct copies of this patent.

Discuss: What different skills could two classmates bring to a design problem? What evidence would you use to explain each person's contribution?

Practice Problems

Think about frequency hopping, invention, and Lamarr's life.

Easy1. In what year were Lamarr and Antheil granted their frequency-hopping patent?

Hint: This was during World War II, several years after she moved to the United States.

Easy2. In our classroom model, what problem can channel hopping help reduce?

Hint: Staying on noisy channel 3 caused every message attempt to be missed in our model.

Medium3. How many years passed between the 1942 frequency-hopping patent and the 1997 EFF Pioneer Award?

Hint: Subtract 1942 from 1997.

Medium4. Which everyday technology uses coordinated frequency hopping?

Hint: Wireless headphones often use this technology to communicate with a phone.

Challenge5. What mechanism inspired Antheil's approach to keeping transmitter and receiver synchronized while hopping frequencies?

Hint: Matching rolls told mechanical pianos which notes to play and when.

Medium6. If a frequency-hopping transmitter switches to a new frequency 50 times per second, how many frequency switches occur in 4 seconds?

Hint: Multiply 50 switches per second by 4 seconds.

Easy7. What stage name did Hedwig Eva Maria Kiesler use as an actor?

Hint: This is the stage name she is remembered by today.

Challenge8. Which evidence identifies Lamarr and Antheil as collaborators on the 1942 invention?

Hint: Look for the original record of the invention, using Lamarr's legal name at the time: Hedy Kiesler Markey.

Teacher discussion guide
Look for both conditions: the devices use the same channel at the same time, and that channel is clear. With noise on channel 3 and synchronized timing, the fixed strategy receives 0/8 and hopping receives 6/8. With noise on channel 1, fixed receives 8/8 and hopping receives 7/8. Hopping is useful under some conditions, not always superior. Older students can express 6/8 as 75% and explain why eight idealized trials cannot establish real-world reliability.
