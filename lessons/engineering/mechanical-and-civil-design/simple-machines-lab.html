Simple Machines Lab

Levers Ramps Pulleys Effort vs. Distance

Simple machines don't remove work — they trade it. Move the fulcrum, the ramp, or the pulleys below and watch the effort gauge change. Every time effort gets easier, notice what you gave up instead: distance, rope, or time. That trade is the whole secret.

Practice Problems

Easy1. If you move a lever's fulcrum closer to the load, what happens to the effort you need?

Hint: Try it in the lab above — slide the fulcrum toward the 🧱 and watch the gauge.

Easy2. A long, shallow ramp is easier to push a box up than a short, steep one. What do you give up to get that easier push?

Hint: A shallow ramp is longer — you travel farther to gain the same height.

Medium3. Adding a second pulley makes lifting a weight feel easier. What do you have to do more of in exchange?

Hint: More pulleys share the load, but the rope has to travel farther to lift the same height.

Medium4. Which simple machine is a wheelchair ramp closest to?

Hint: It's a slanted surface that trades distance for less climbing effort — exactly what a ramp does.

Challenge5. Every simple machine in this lab makes one thing easier by making something else bigger. In your own words, what is that "something else" usually — for a lever, a ramp, and a pulley?

Hint: Less force almost always means moving through more distance — that's the trade-off behind every simple machine.
