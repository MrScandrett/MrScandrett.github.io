Every lesson in this module has been one piece of the engineering process: reading parts, wiring circuits, tuning feedback loops, sizing structures. This capstone walks the whole loop at once, against one real, constraint-driven brief — and asks you to defend the tradeoffs you make, not just pick an answer.

The Design Loop

Real engineering isn't a straight line from idea to finished product — it's a loop. You define the problem, build a model of a solution, prototype it, test it against reality, and use what you learn to go back and improve it. Work through all five stages below for one brief: a drone rig built to drop emergency supplies to a stranded hiker.

Studio: Design a Rescue-Supply Drone Rig

Brief: A hiker is stranded on a ridge no vehicle can reach. Your rig must hang below a delivery drone and release a supply pack remotely, without landing.

  • 📦 Payload (supply pack) must not exceed 500 g
  • ⚖️ Total rig weight (frame + release mechanism, empty) must not exceed 700 g so the drone can still carry the payload within its lift limit
  • 💥 The rig must survive a 2 m drop test onto rock without breaking
  • 📡 The release must be triggerable remotely — no manual pin-pulling

Choose a frame material. Every material trades weight against durability.

Practice Problems

Easy1. Put the design loop in order: Test, Define, Iterate, Model, Prototype.

Hint: You can't build a model of a problem you haven't defined yet, and you can't test something that doesn't exist.

Easy2. Why is "Iterate" a loop back to earlier stages instead of a final step?

Hint: Real designs are rarely right on the first try — that's exactly why it's a loop, not a line.

Medium3. In this studio, why does the brief cap the empty rig weight at 700 g instead of just capping the payload?

Hint: A drone doesn't distinguish "rig weight" from "payload weight" — it just has one total weight limit.

Medium4. If a lightweight frame fails the drop test, what's the most useful next step?

Hint: This is the loop in action — a failed Test sends you back to Model to weigh a new tradeoff, not to give up.

Challenge5. Why does the brief require a remote release instead of allowing a simple manual pin a rescuer pulls by hand?

Hint: Re-read the brief — the drone isn't landing, so nobody is there to pull a pin.

How to use this studio Work through the 5 stage tabs in order. Model and Prototype set up your design; Test tells you if it survives the brief; Iterate is where you reflect.
There's more than one valid design Several frame/mechanism combinations can pass — the practice problems and reflection ask you to defend the tradeoff, not just find "the" answer.
1. Define What problem, and what constraints must any solution satisfy?
2. Model Choose the pieces of a solution and weigh their tradeoffs on paper.
3. Prototype Commit to a real, buildable combination.
4. Test Check the prototype against every constraint from Define — honestly.
5. Iterate Use what Test revealed to improve the design, then loop back.
