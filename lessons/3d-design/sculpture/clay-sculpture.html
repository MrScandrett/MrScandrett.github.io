Build, join, and carve clay into a figurine, portrait bust, or relief. Work the same form through wet clay, leather-hard detail, and firing.

Study the makers

  • Greek Tanagra figurines, late 4th century BCE onward. Makers pressed clay in two-part molds. They could attach heads and arms in different positions to vary the pose. Start with a Figurine blank and build the same form into two poses. Excavations in the cemeteries of Tanagra yielded many such figures. What might a familiar gesture have meant to someone choosing a figure to bury? Greek Lands and Letters, an early account of the finds.
  • Qin China, late 3rd century BCE. The terracotta army was buried in pits at the tomb complex of the first emperor, Qin Shihuang. Its life-size figures were made in stages. A whole formation conveys imperial power, while a single face invites you to look for an individual. Look at how the armor and facial features change the silhouette of each figure. Work the major masses first, then incise the details.
  • Renaissance Florence. Fired clay reliefs could be gilded or painted. Start with a Relief slab and make a small scene where the raised shape casts a readable shadow. Madonna and Child from the School of Donatello at The Met.

Read the broken edge

This little Cypriot figure has a molded front and a roughly worked back. The angle of the break led the museum to suggest that it once decorated a vessel. The break is evidence you can point to. The missing vessel is a reconstruction. Sketch two different supports that could fit this edge. Which extra fragment would settle the question?

Inside the workshop. A mold stores a shape in reverse. Push soft clay into a hollow cheek in the mold and the copy comes out with a raised cheek. A maker can repeat that face, then change its gaze or add a handmade arm. Start two figures with the same preset. Change just the tilt of the head. Which one seems to be listening?

How does earth become a devotional image?

The attribution “School of Donatello” connects this relief to an artistic tradition in Florence. It leaves the individual maker unnamed. Look at the space between the two faces. How does the artist tell a story about mother and child within a slab only a few layers deep?

Luca della Robbia, another Florentine sculptor, experimented with glaze over modeled clay. Vasari, writing the following century, tells the story as a search for a durable surface. Compare three ways of finishing a relief: colored pigment changes what you see, gold leaf catches light, and glaze fuses into a glassy surface in firing. Which finish would make a shallow fold easiest to read across a room?

Make the comparison. Model two overlapping hands on the relief slab. Leave one almost level with the ground and build the other higher. Turn the slab until the projected shadow tells you which hand is in front. Public-domain reading: Giorgio Vasari, Life of Luca della Robbia, in the public-domain English translation.

Studio challenge

  1. Block out the form. Start with a lump, slab, figurine, or bust. Attach big shapes with Join clay. On real pieces you would score and slip joins before pressing them together.
  2. Model and refine. Build up cheeks, a nose or cloth folds with the thumb tool. Orbit to check the silhouette from the side. Cut with the loop for hollows and lines, then blend seams with the rib. Right-drag to look around without changing tools.
  3. Finish a maquette. Once leather-hard, sharpen a few details with the loop. Dry and fire your piece. Compare two versions using the same starting form.

The firing preview uses a fixed shrinkage estimate for each clay body and previews the chosen finish. Actual results depend on the clay, glaze and firing process.

Take your model out of the studio

  • Keep editing. Save a .clay project to keep your geometry, clay body, and firing stage. Open it here to continue.
  • Bring in your own mesh. Open a closed STL, OBJ, 3MF, or self-contained GLB. The studio turns it into editable clay you can add to, scoop, and smooth. Imported surfaces lose textures and may lose very thin details.
  • Print a study. Export STL and open it in your slicer. Confirm the size, thin walls, and any supports with the layer preview.
  • Make a game prop. Export GLB for a Y-up model in metres with its clay color and finish, or OBJ for geometry you can texture yourself.
