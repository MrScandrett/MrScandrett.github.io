    Try a tiny discovery

    Curious? Here’s how the map works.

    Why “fifths”?

    Count C–D–E–F–G: five letter names. C to G is seven semitones. Each clockwise step moves up a perfect fifth. Moving left goes up a fourth instead.

    What do ♯ and ♭ mean?

    A sharp raises a note one semitone; a flat lowers it one. From C, moving right adds sharps in the order F C G D A E B; moving left adds flats in the order B E A D G C F. The key signature tells you which to use.

    Why do chords have numbers?

    I means a chord built on note 1; IV starts on note 4; V starts on note 5. Uppercase means major, lowercase means minor. Change the key and the chord names move, but their relationships stay. That’s transposition.

    Two names, one piano pitch

    F♯ and G♭ sound the same on a modern piano but spell their scales differently. Try the spelling switch at the bottom of the circle. Sound here uses 12-tone equal temperament; the minor scale is natural minor.
