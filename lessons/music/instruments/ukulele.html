Ukulele: First Chords & Strums

The rows follow string order G–C–E–A. Open means fret 0. Strum sweeps from G to A.

Shapes

    Choose a fret to pluck its note, or load C and strum all four strings.

    ← Music lessons

    First sound on a ukulele

    1. Hold the body against your chest. With the neck pointing left, the string nearest your face is G. Pluck G, C, E and A one at a time.
    2. For C, use your ring finger just behind fret 3 of the A string. Let G, C and E ring open. Brush gently across all four strings toward the floor.
    3. For F, use your middle finger on fret 2 of G and index on fret 1 of E. For G7, keep your index in place, move your middle finger to fret 2 of C and add your ring finger to fret 2 of A. For Am, press only fret 2 of G with your middle finger.
    4. Listen to the first progression. Count four beats aloud on each chord and strum down on each beat. In Practice, strum each shape once to advance. On a real ukulele, slow down until each change lands on beat one.

    Why the notes change

    A plucked string vibrates. Pressing behind a fret shortens its vibrating length, raising the pitch by one semitone per fret. In this high-G tuning, the open strings are G4, C4, E4 and A4. The C string is the lowest in pitch even though G comes first in string order.

    Hawaiian makers developed the ukulele from small Portuguese string instruments brought to Hawaiʻi in the late nineteenth century. Explore the Smithsonian’s ukulele history.

    Show what you learned

    Play C–F–G7–C twice with a steady count. Then pluck G7 one string at a time. Which finger can stay in place as you move from F to G7?
