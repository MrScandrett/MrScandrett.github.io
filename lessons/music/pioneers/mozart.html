Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart

Salzburg & Vienna Classical, 1756–1791 Child Prodigy & Master Over 600 Works (K. 1–K. 626)

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (1756–1791) toured the courts of Europe as a small child, wrote his first symphony at about eight, and in a life of only 35 years produced operas, symphonies, concertos and chamber music that still fill concert halls. His music sounds effortless — clear tunes, balanced phrases — but underneath is careful, logical design.

Who Was Mozart?

Mozart was born in Salzburg (now in Austria) on January 27, 1756. His father, Leopold, was a court violinist who published a well-known violin method the same year. Wolfgang and his older sister Maria Anna, called Nannerl, were both extraordinary keyboard players. By five Wolfgang was making up short pieces, which his father wrote down.

From 1762 the family spent years on the road, performing for royalty in Munich, Vienna, Paris and London. In London the eight-year-old met Johann Christian Bach, J. S. Bach’s youngest son, and wrote his first symphonies. On trips to Italy as a teenager he studied opera and counterpoint; in Rome he is reported to have written down a famous choral piece, Allegri’s Miserere, from memory after hearing it.

Back in Salzburg, Mozart worked for the archbishop but felt underpaid and confined. In 1781 he was dismissed and moved to Vienna to live as a freelance musician — teaching, performing his own piano concertos, and selling his music. He married Constanze Weber in 1782. In the next decade he wrote the operas The Marriage of Figaro, Don Giovanni and The Magic Flute, his last symphonies, and the serenade Eine kleine Nachtmusik, which he entered in his own catalogue of works on August 10, 1787.

Mozart often earned well but spent freely, and money worries followed him. In late 1791 he fell ill while working on a Requiem Mass, which he left unfinished. He died in Vienna on December 5, 1791, aged 35. In 1862 Ludwig von Köchel published a catalogue numbering all of Mozart’s works in order — which is why his pieces have “K.” numbers, like K. 525.

A Short, Crowded Life

  1. 1756 Born January 27 in Salzburg.
  2. 1761 Composes his first short keyboard pieces, written down by his father.
  3. 1762–66 Family tour across Europe, including Paris and London.
  4. 1764 Meets J. C. Bach in London; writes his first symphony.
  5. 1770 In Rome, writes out Allegri’s Miserere from memory (as reported in family letters).
  6. 1781 Leaves the archbishop’s service and settles in Vienna.
  7. 1783 Writes the Piano Sonata in A major with the “Rondo alla turca” finale (date approximate).
  8. 1786 The Marriage of Figaro premieres in Vienna.
  9. 1787 Completes Eine kleine Nachtmusik on August 10.
  10. 1791 The Magic Flute premieres; Mozart dies December 5, leaving his Requiem unfinished.

Question and Answer: Balanced Phrases

Classical-era music (roughly 1750–1820) prized clarity and balance. Composers built melodies from phrases that pair up like a question and an answer: the first phrase ends feeling open, the second ends feeling closed. Music theorists call these an antecedent and a consequent.

Eine kleine Nachtmusik shows this in just four bars. The opening rises through the home chord, G major. The answer outlines the dominant chord D7 — the chord that most strongly pulls back to G. When the music finally lands on G, the ear hears it as “home.”

The “Rondo alla turca” is a rondo: a main theme (A) that keeps returning between contrasting sections — A B A C A — a little like a song with a chorus.

I → V → I
I (tonic), G major: G – B – D · feels stable, like home
V7 (dominant seventh), D7: D – F♯ – A – C · feels tense and wants to move
Resolution: F♯ rises to G and C falls to B, landing back on the tonic

Home → away → home is the basic plot of Classical harmony, from a four-bar phrase to a whole symphony.

Did Mozart really invent a dice game for composing?
A “Musical Dice Game” was published under Mozart’s name in 1792, after his death: you roll dice to pick each of 16 bars from a table of choices. Whether Mozart actually wrote it is uncertain. With 11 possible rolls for each bar, it can produce about 1116 — tens of quadrillions — different minuets.
What does K. 525 mean?
K stands for Köchel, the scholar who catalogued Mozart’s works in rough order of composition in 1862. K. 525 is the 525th entry.
Was Mozart really a rival of Salieri?
They were both successful composers in Vienna and sometimes competed for commissions, but the story that Salieri poisoned Mozart is a legend with no evidence behind it.

Who put the “K.” in Mozart’s music?

Financial independence: Ludwig von Köchel (1800–1877) was an Austrian educator and wide-ranging scholar of law, botany, mineralogy, literature and music. After tutoring the children of Archduke Karl of Austria, he received a generous pension in 1842. It allowed him to live as an independent scholar and pursue research without needing it to turn a profit.

A “Mozart worshiper”: Köchel was devoted to bringing order to Mozart’s legacy. The composer’s manuscripts and editions were scattered across Europe; some works were missing, appeared in several versions or had been falsely published under Mozart’s name. Köchel built on earlier catalogues and searched scores, letters and archives to identify the works and place them in order.

A scientific approach to art: Köchel applied a natural scientist’s habit of classification to music. Over roughly eleven years, he organized 626 known works into the first systematic chronological and thematic catalogue of a major composer, published in 1862. He wanted its sequence to reveal Mozart’s growth from child prodigy to mature master. That is why the scores below carry labels such as K. 525.

Two keysA and B are in A minor (no sharps). C, D, E and the coda are in A major (three sharps: F♯, C♯, G♯). The same notes feel stormy, then sunny, then stormy again.

Mozart's Key Contributions

✍️
Over 600 Works in 35 Years

The Köchel catalogue lists more than 600 works: symphonies, concertos, operas, chamber music, masses and songs.

🎭
Opera That Shows Character

The Marriage of Figaro, Don Giovanni and The Magic Flute use music to show what each person on stage is thinking and feeling.

🎹
The Piano Concerto

His 27 piano concertos turned the form into a conversation between soloist and orchestra. He often played the solo part himself.

🎼
The Last Three Symphonies

Symphonies 39, 40 and 41 ("Jupiter") were all written in about six weeks in the summer of 1788.

⚖️
Classical Balance

Clear question-and-answer phrases and well-proportioned forms made his music the model of the Classical style.

💼
Going Freelance

In 1781 he left the archbishop’s service in Salzburg to earn his living in Vienna by teaching, performing and publishing.

Mozart's Legacy Today

Mozart wrote masterpieces in every major form of his day, and his operas are still among the most performed in the world. Beethoven, fourteen years younger, went to Vienna hoping to study with him; Tchaikovsky called Mozart his musical idol and wrote a suite based on his music.

His fame as a child prodigy has made “Mozart” a shorthand for early genius, but his letters show how hard he worked: studying other composers, revising, and practising. The music that sounds effortless was built on years of disciplined training — and on the structure of question, answer and return that you can see in four bars of the score above.

Medium5. Which pattern describes a rondo?

Hint: The main theme keeps returning between other sections.

Medium6. Eine kleine Nachtmusik is K. 525 and was finished in 1787. Mozart died in 1791. How many years later?

Hint: 1791 − 1787.

Challenge7. The Musical Dice Game picks each of 16 bars from 11 choices. About how many different minuets are possible? Enter the exponent n in 11ⁿ.

Hint: 11 choices for bar 1 × 11 for bar 2 × … sixteen times.
