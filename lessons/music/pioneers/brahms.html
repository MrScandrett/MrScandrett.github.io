Life and context

Johannes Brahms (1833–1897).

Brahms was born in Hamburg and later made Vienna his home. His symphonies, chamber music, piano works, and songs combine expressive Romantic language with a close study of earlier musical forms.

Developing variation

Developing variation means generating new material by changing features of an existing idea. Brahms also layered rhythmic groupings, allowing different accents to make a steady beat feel unsettled.

Guided listening

Choose a performance of one of these works. Listen once for its overall character, then again for the detail in the prompt.

  • Symphony No. 4: listen for variation in the final movement.
  • Intermezzo in A major, Op. 118 No. 2: follow small ideas as their context changes.
  • Hungarian Dance No. 5: listen for changes in pace and character.

Practice and reflection

  1. Play both sound examples. Describe one feature that changes and one that stays the same.
  2. How much can you change a musical idea before you hear it as a new one?
  3. Create a four-note idea at the piano. Make two versions using today’s musical idea, then explain your choices to a partner.
Check your listening

Compare a short idea with a version that changes its ending. Identify the exact notes that preserve the family resemblance. A useful answer names a feature you can hear and explains its effect. More than one interpretation is possible.
