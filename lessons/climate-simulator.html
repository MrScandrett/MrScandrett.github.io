About This Model

🌿 Carbon Cycle

CO₂ and CH₄ trap outgoing infrared radiation. The model uses the classic Myhre et al. (1998) radiative forcing formula: ΔF = 5.35 × ln(C/C₀) for CO₂, scaled by the methane forcing contribution. IPCC AR6 now uses a refined version of this relationship (Etminan et al. 2016, via Meinshausen et al. 2020) — the logarithmic form here is a close, teachable approximation of it. Forests remove ~2.6 Gt CO₂/yr — deforestation reduces this sink.

☀️ Energy Balance

Earth's temperature is set by the balance of incoming solar energy and outgoing thermal radiation. Ice and snow reflect sunlight (high albedo). As ice melts, the darker ocean absorbs more heat — a positive feedback loop that amplifies warming.

🌊 Ocean Systems

Oceans absorb ~90% of excess heat and ~30% of CO₂ emissions, slowing surface warming but causing acidification. As pH drops below 8.0, coral reefs and shellfish are threatened. Warmer seas fuel more intense hurricanes and alter global currents.

Go Deeper

This simulator's weather and convection patterns are a simplified, hand-tuned model. Real climate scientists train machine learning models on physical simulations like the Rayleigh-Bénard convection runs in The Well — a free 15TB collection of fluid dynamics, turbulence, and other physics simulation datasets released by Polymathic AI for training and testing ML models on physical systems.
