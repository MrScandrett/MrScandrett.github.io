French Language Foundations

Romance Family 5 Diacritics Nasal Vowels Liaison & Elision Negation Sandwich (ne...pas)

French is a descendant of Vulgar Latin reshaped across two millennia by Celtic and Germanic influences. It is famous for its melodious continuity—where words connect smoothly across word boundaries through liaison and enchaînement. Explore its accent marks, nasal phonemes, grammatical gender, and structural rules.

Architecture

How French Works: The Four Pillars

1. Gender & Agreement

Every noun is masculine (le/un) or feminine (la/une). Adjectives and articles change shape to match.

2. The 5 Accents

Accents are not decorations: é, è, ê, ë, and ç fundamentally change vowel sounds and preserve history.

3. Silent Consonants

Final consonants (s, t, d, x, p, z) are usually silent, except when linked to a vowel in the next word (liaison).

4. Negation Sandwich

To say "not", the verb is hugged by two words: ne [verb] pas (e.g., Je ne sais pas).

Progression Pathway

Roadmap for Future French Lessons

LEVEL 1 Present Tense & Regular Verbs

Conjugating the three verb groups (-er, -ir, -re), plus irregular pillars: être (to be), avoir (to have), faire (to do/make), and aller (to go).

LEVEL 2 Passé Composé & Imparfait

Narrating past events using auxiliary verbs with past participles, and contrasting completed events with habitual background descriptions.

LEVEL 3 Subjunctive & Authentic Literature

Expressing doubt, desire, necessity with the subjunctive mood; reading Victor Hugo, Albert Camus, and Francophone global literature.
