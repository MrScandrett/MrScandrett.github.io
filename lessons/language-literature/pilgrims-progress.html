The Pilgrim’s Progress

John Bunyan’s dream of a journey from this world to the next

A man named Christian carries a heavy burden and leaves his home to find a better city. Along the way he meets characters whose names tell you exactly what they are: Obstinate, Pliable, Faithful, Giant Despair. Bunyan, a self-taught preacher from Bedford, wrote the story in prison and published it in 1678. For generations it was among the most widely read books in English after the Bible.

AllegoryPersonificationPlain styleJourney structure
Big Idea

Bunyan makes an inner life visible. Fear, temptation, despair, and hope become places and people, so the reader can watch a spiritual struggle unfold like an adventure.

Quick Facts

AuthorJohn Bunyan, born Elstow, England, 1628; died London, 1688
PublishedPart One in 1678; Part Two (the journey of Christian’s wife Christiana) in 1684
FormA prose allegory framed as a dream
BackgroundBunyan was a tinker (a mender of pots) who preached without a license and was jailed for about twelve years
LegacyTranslated into well over a hundred languages; it inspired later writers from Thackeray to Louisa May Alcott

How the Book Begins

Bunyan’s text is in the public domain. Here is the opening, lightly modernized.

As I walked through the wilderness of this world, I lighted on a certain place where was a Den, and I laid me down in that place to sleep: and, as I slept, I dreamed a dream.Opening of Part One. The “Den” is the jail where Bunyan wrote.

A dream frame

Bunyan tells the story as a dream. Dreams let anything be symbolic, so a swamp can mean despair without an explanation.

Names as meaning

In allegory a name tells you what a person represents. Obstinate is stubborn, Pliable follows the crowd, and Mr. Worldly Wiseman gives clever, comfortable advice.

Plain style

Bunyan wrote in the language of the King James Bible and everyday English, so a farmworker could follow it aloud.

A road story

Every chapter is a place to reach, a danger to survive, or a person to meet. The structure is a quest with no need for complex plot.

Follow Christian’s Road

Nine stops on the journey. For each, ask what the place means and where you might meet it today.

What happens

Christian reads a book (the Bible) and becomes deeply troubled. Evangelist tells him to flee. His neighbors Obstinate and Pliable follow for a short way.

What it means

Waking up to a problem you cannot ignore, and leaving comfort behind.

Today

The moment you realize you can’t keep going the way you are.

What to notice

Christian’s family thinks he has lost his mind. Bunyan shows that changing course can cost you your closest relationships.

What happens

Christian falls into a bog and sinks under his burden. Pliable turns back. A man named Help pulls Christian out.

What it means

The despair that follows the first hopeful step.

Today

Discouragement early in any hard project.

What to notice

The Slough is right at the start, and that is the point. The first hurdle is often the mood of “what have I gotten myself into?”

What happens

Mr. Worldly Wiseman offers a comfortable shortcut through the village of Morality. Christian nearly takes it, but Evangelist steers him back to the Wicket Gate.

What it means

Advice that promises relief without change.

Today

Quick fixes and life hacks that skip the hard part.

What to notice

Worldly Wiseman is friendly and reasonable. The most dangerous advice in the book sounds sensible.

What happens

At the Interpreter’s House Christian is shown pictures that explain what he sees. At the cross, his burden falls from his back and rolls away.

What it means

Relief and forgiveness, and the end of carrying guilt.

Today

The feeling when a long-held worry is finally resolved.

What to notice

This is the emotional midpoint of Part One, but the journey is not over. Bunyan insists that relief is not arrival.

What happens

Christian climbs a steep hill instead of taking easy side paths. At the Palace Beautiful he rests among friends and is given armor.

What it means

Perseverance, and the value of a community that equips you.

Today

The hard middle stretch of anything, and the people who keep you going.

What to notice

The lions beside the palace path look terrifying, but they are chained. Bunyan says many fears turn out to be chained lions.

What happens

Christian fights Apollyon, a scaly monster, and wins. Then he passes through the Valley of the Shadow of Death.

What it means

Temptation and fear, met head-on.

Today

Facing a fear you cannot walk around.

What to notice

Bunyan mixes epic fighting with quiet fear. In the dark valley the greatest danger is the whispering in Christian’s own mind.

What happens

The pilgrims pass through a town-wide market where everything is for sale. They refuse to buy and are put on trial. Faithful is executed, and Christian escapes with a new companion, Hopeful.

What it means

A world of status, spectacle, and pressure to conform.

Today

Advertising, influencer culture, and peer pressure.

What to notice

Thackeray named his 1848 novel Vanity Fair after this scene. Notice that the crowd punishes the pilgrims mostly for being different.

What happens

Cutting through By-path Meadow for an easier walk, Christian and Hopeful are captured by Giant Despair. After days of abuse, Christian remembers a key called Promise.

What it means

The lowest point: despair after a wrong choice, and the reminder that hope can be remembered.

Today

Being stuck after a mistake, and finding your way back.

What to notice

The key was in Christian’s pocket all along. Bunyan says despair blinds you to what you already have.

What happens

The pilgrims pass the Delectable Mountains and the Enchanted Ground, then cross a deep river. Christian nearly fails, but Hopeful helps. They reach the Celestial City.

What it means

Death and the reward that lies beyond it.

Today

The last stretch of any long effort, when you need help most.

What to notice

Even at the end, Christian needs a companion. The book keeps returning to the idea that nobody makes the trip alone.

Decode the Allegory

Bunyan’s names and places are meant to be read as symbols. Choose the best meaning for each.

Why the Book Lasted

These features help explain why readers kept coming back.

Ordinary hero

Christian is a common man with doubts and failures. He is not a knight.

Vivid dialogue

The characters talk like real people, arguing, joking, and complaining. It reads like a play.

Inner life as adventure

Feelings become places you can walk through. A reader can see despair as a swamp and temptation as a shortcut.

Influence

In Little Women the March sisters act out the book as children, and Alcott’s first part is built around it. Thackeray borrowed the title of Vanity Fair, and Pilgrim’s Progress shaped many later journey stories.

A shared text

For generations it sat beside the Bible in many homes, and it was read aloud to families and to those who could not read.

Part Two

In 1684 Bunyan sent Christian’s wife Christiana and their children on the same road, and it shows that the journey looks different for a family.

Bunyan’s Life in Brief

  1. 1628: Born in Elstow near Bedford, the son of a tinker who mended pots and pans.
  2. 1644–47: Serves as a soldier during the English Civil War.
  3. 1650s: Undergoes a long spiritual struggle, joins a Bedford congregation, and becomes a preacher.
  4. 1660: After the monarchy is restored, is arrested for preaching without a license and jailed for about twelve years.
  5. 1666: Publishes his spiritual autobiography, Grace Abounding.
  6. 1678: Publishes The Pilgrim’s Progress, Part One.
  7. 1684: Publishes Part Two.
  8. 1688: Dies in London and is buried in Bunhill Fields.

Try It Yourself

Write and think in allegory.

Name a character

Invent three characters whose names show what they are (like Pliable or Talkative). Give each a line of dialogue.

Map your road

Draw a road with five stops that represent the stages of learning something difficult. Name each place.

Compare

Compare Christian’s journey with Dante’s. Both are lost at the start. Who guides each one, and what does each journey change?

Modern Vanity Fair

Describe a modern Vanity Fair. What is sold there, and what would a pilgrim have to refuse?
