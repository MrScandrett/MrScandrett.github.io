Russian Language Foundations

Slavic Family 33 Cyrillic Letters Hard & Soft Consonants 6 Grammatical Cases No Articles / Zero Copula

Russian is the largest native language in Europe and the primary member of the East Slavic branch. Written in the Cyrillic script adapted from Byzantine Greek in the 9th century, Russian operates with six expressive cases, paired hard and soft consonants, and an economy of expression that needs neither articles ("the", "a") nor the present-tense verb "to be."

Architecture

How Russian Works: The Four Pillars

1. Cyrillic Alphabet

33 letters: some look and sound like English (А, О, М), but beware of False Friends (В=[v], Н=[n], Р=[r], С=[s]).

2. Hard vs Soft

Nearly every consonant has a hard (plain) and soft (palatalized) counterpart, altering the word's acoustic feel and meaning.

3. Six Cases

Nominative, Accusative, Genitive, Dative, Instrumental, and Prepositional cases tag the exact syntactic role of each noun.

4. Zero Copula & No Articles

There is no "the" or "a", and "to be" is skipped in the present: Я студент literally means "I student" = "I am a student"!

Progression Pathway

Roadmap for Future Russian Lessons

LEVEL 1 Cyrillic Cursive & Vowel Reduction

Reading Russian handwriting, mastering unstressed vowel reduction (akan'ye: unstressed "o" softens to "a"), and noun genders (он, она, оно).

LEVEL 2 Case Endings & Verbal Aspect

The six-case declension system for adjectives and nouns, plus perfective vs. imperfective verb pairs (process vs. completed result).

LEVEL 3 Verbs of Motion & Russian Classics

Multi-directional vs. unidirectional verbs of motion (идти/ходить); reading Pushkin, Tolstoy, Chekhov, and Dostoevsky in original Russian.
