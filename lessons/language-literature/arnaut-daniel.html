Language & Literature · Medieval poetry

Arnaut Daniel

A troubadour made six words travel through a poem. Follow their route, then try the constraint yourself.

Guiding question: When a word returns in a new place, does its meaning stay the same?

Meet the poet

Time and place

Arnaut Daniel was an Occitan-language troubadour active around the late twelfth and early thirteenth centuries in southern France. The exact dates of his life are uncertain.

The art

Troubadours composed lyric verse, often for performance. Arnaut became associated with trobar clus, a densely wrought style that rewards slow listening and rereading.

The afterlife

In Purgatorio XXVI, Dante has Guido Guinizzelli praise Arnaut as a master of writing in the vernacular. Arnaut then speaks in Occitan within Dante's Italian poem.

Listen and discuss: What can a poet do with a spoken language that a formal language might discourage? Why might Dante let Arnaut answer in his own language?

Read the real poem first

Arnaut's Lo ferm voler qu'el cor m'intra is the early model for the sestina. Read an edition or translation alongside this page. Track the six words at the ends of its lines before you use the workshop below. Its original Occitan words and their meanings need care in translation; the English words in the workshop are an original classroom example, not Arnaut's text.

The six-word workshop

A sestina has six six-line stanzas. The same six end words return, but in a new order each time. A short three-line envoi gathers them at the end. Select a word to follow it; use the stanza buttons to see the rearrangement.

      Put your words into the pattern

      Choose six concrete words from a place you know. Replace the sample words, then write six lines that end with the displayed words. Advance to stanza 2 and see what new connections the required order suggests.

      Small start: “The classroom looks toward the window.” Ask what the same window could mean after five other images have appeared.

      Discuss: Which word became hardest to use? Where did the rule lead you to an idea you would not have chosen on your own?
