German Language Foundations

Germanic Family Sister to English All Nouns Capitalized Umlauts & Eszett Verb-Second (V2) Engine

German is the most widely spoken native tongue in the European Union and the closest major linguistic sister to English. Prized for intellectual precision in science, engineering, and philosophy, German operates like a finely tuned machine: every noun is capitalized, compound words assemble modular concepts, and the verb sits rigidly in slot number two.

Architecture

How German Works: The Four Pillars

1. Capitalize All Nouns

Every noun in German starts with a capital letter: der Hund (the Dog), das Buch (the Book), die Freiheit (the Freedom).

2. Verb-Second (V2)

In main clauses, the conjugated verb is always in position #2: "Heute gehe ich..." (Today go I...).

3. Modular Compounds

German builds ideas like Lego bricks: Kühl (cool) + Schrank (cupboard) = Kühlschrank (refrigerator)!

4. English Cognates

Because English and German are sisters, thousands of words match: water/Wasser, apple/Apfel, house/Haus, friend/Freund.

Umlaut Ä (/ɛ/ or /e:/)

Pronounced like "e" in bed or "ai" in air. Example: Äpfel (apples), Käse (cheese).
