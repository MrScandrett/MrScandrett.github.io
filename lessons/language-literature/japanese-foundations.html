Japanese Language Foundations

Japonic Family Kana & Kanji SOV Word Order Topic Particles (は/が) Moraic Rhythm

Japanese is an agglutinative language with an exceptionally logical structure: verbs always anchor the end of the clause, grammatical relations are tagged with precise particles, and sounds are metered not in syllables but in uniform time units called mora. Explore the tripartite writing system, phonology, and sentence architecture.

Architecture

How Japanese Works: The Four Pillars

Before memorizing vocabulary, understand the engine underneath the language:

1. SOV Order

Verbs always finish the sentence: Subject + Object + Verb. Information flows from broad context to final action.

2. Particles (助詞)

Postpositions like は (wa), を (o), and に (ni) tag the syntactic role of whatever word precedes them.

3. Three Scripts

Hiragana for grammar & native words; Katakana for loanwords; Kanji for conceptual roots.

4. Mora Timing

Every beat (mora) receives equal duration: to-ky-o-u is 4 beats, not 2 english syllables. Pure, predictable cadence.
