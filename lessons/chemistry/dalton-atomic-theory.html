Chemistry pioneer · Models · Ratios

Dalton turns atoms into a testable idea

John Dalton connected measurements of gases and compounds to a bold model: matter is made of tiny particles, and compounds form when particles combine in simple whole-number ratios.

Predict: If one compound uses one carbon particle for every oxygen particle, what should happen when a second compound uses one carbon for every two oxygens?

1. A model built across decades

  1. 1797–1799 · Proust Joseph Proust argues that a pure compound has a fixed composition, challenging the idea that mixtures can vary continuously.
  2. 1803–1808 · Dalton Dalton proposes that elements contain characteristic atoms and that compounds form from small whole-number combinations.
  3. 1808 · A table of atoms His New System of Chemical Philosophy publishes circle symbols and relative atomic weights—an early attempt to make invisible particles countable.
  4. 1811 · Avogadro Amedeo Avogadro distinguishes atoms from molecules and explains why reacting gases show simple volume ratios.
  5. 1897–1913 · The atom opens Thomson's electron and Rutherford's nucleus show that Dalton's solid-ball picture is incomplete, while the particulate foundation survives.

3. Build the particle explanation

Carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide contain the same two elements, but their oxygen counts compare as 1:2. The small circles are a model of the ratio, not a photograph of an atom.

Choose the evidence that actually tests the model.

4. Reflection: models have limits

  1. Draw a compound with a 1:3 ratio.
  2. What observation would make you revise your drawing?
  3. Why is “an atom is a tiny solid ball” useful in one problem but limited in another?
