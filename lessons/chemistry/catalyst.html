Chemistry · Lesson 04

Catalysts & Activation Energy

Reaction Kinetics Activation Energy Arrhenius Equation Enzymes

Why can a reaction release energy and still happen slowly? Reactants must first cross an energy barrier. Explore how a catalyst changes the route, compare rates at the same temperature, and explain why faster does not mean more energy released.

1. A downhill reaction can still need a push

Breaking and rearranging bonds requires an initial investment of energy. The highest point on an energy diagram represents a transition state: an unstable arrangement between reactants and products. The barrier measured from the reactants to that point is the activation energy, Ea.

Before you test: If a catalyst lowers the hill, which should change: the speed of reaction, the energy released, or both? Make a prediction, then compare the two paths below.

2. Same destination, a different route

Every chemical reaction has an energy barrier called the activation energy (Ea) — the minimum energy reactants need to start reacting. Think of it as a hill: reactants must climb the hill before they can roll down to become products.

k = A · e−Ea/RT
k = reaction rate constant
A = pre-exponential factor (units match k; includes collision and orientation effects)
Ea = activation energy (J/mol)
R = gas constant ≈ 8.314 J/mol·K
T = temperature (Kelvin)

What a Catalyst Does

A catalyst provides an alternate reaction pathway with a lower activation energy. It does not change the energy of the reactants or products — the overall energy change (ΔH) is identical. The start and end are the same; only the route is different.

Key rule: A catalyst participates in intermediate steps and is regenerated in the net reaction. It does not change ΔH or the equilibrium position at a fixed temperature. It speeds the approach to equilibrium in both directions. Real catalysts can lose activity through poisoning or degradation.

Watch the catalyst return

Reactant + catalyst → intermediate → product + catalyst

The catalyst temporarily binds or reacts, helps bonds rearrange, then becomes available for another cycle. Enzymes are biological catalysts, usually proteins. Their active site binds a substrate and stabilizes the transition state. The lock-and-key picture is a starting analogy; induced fit recognizes that the enzyme can change shape as it binds.

Real-World Applications

🚗
Catalytic Converters Platinum and palladium catalysts in car exhausts convert toxic CO and NOₓ gases into less directly toxic CO₂ and N₂ (CO₂ still contributes to climate change) at exhaust temperatures.
🌾
Haber Process An iron catalyst converts nitrogen (N₂) and hydrogen (H₂) into ammonia (NH₃) at ~450°C and 200 atm — a major source of nitrogen for fertilizers.
🧬
Enzyme Biology Digestive enzymes (amylase, lipase), DNA polymerase, and thousands of other enzymes run the chemistry of every living cell.
⚡
Fuel Cells Platinum catalysts enable hydrogen fuel cells to efficiently split H₂ and react it with O₂ to produce electricity, water, and heat.

5. Check your understanding

Use the Arrhenius equation: k = A·e−Ea/RT. R = 8.314 J/mol·K.

Easy1. A catalyst is consumed during the reaction. True or False?

Hint: A catalyst provides a lower-energy pathway but is regenerated — it is never consumed.

Easy2. What does a catalyst lower to speed up a reaction?

Hint: A catalyst creates an alternate pathway with a lower energy barrier for reactants to cross.

Medium3. The Arrhenius equation shows k = A·e−Ea/RT. If Ea is halved by a catalyst at T = 300 K, would the reaction rate increase or decrease?

Hint: A smaller exponent (−Ea/RT becomes less negative) means e−Ea/RT gets larger, so k increases.

Medium4. Enzymes are biological catalysts. What is the region on an enzyme where the substrate binds?

Hint: The lock-and-key model: the substrate (key) fits into the enzyme's active site (lock).

Challenge5. Without a catalyst Ea = 80 kJ/mol; with a catalyst Ea = 50 kJ/mol. At T = 300 K, by approximately what factor does the rate increase? Use k ∝ e−Ea/RT.

Hint: ratio = e(80000−50000)/(8.314×300) = e12.0 ≈ 167,000
