Physics · Lesson 09

The Falling Coil

Electromagnetism Induction Lenz's Law Motion → Current

Drop a conducting coil through a magnetic field and something remarkable happens — the changing flux induces a current, and that current creates a magnetic field that opposes the motion. Motion becomes electricity, and electricity pushes back.

The Big Idea

When a coil of wire moves through a magnetic field (or a magnet moves through a coil), the number of magnetic field lines passing through the coil changes. That change is called a change in magnetic flux. According to Faraday's Law, any change in flux induces a voltage — and if the circuit is closed, that voltage drives a current.

Lenz's Law: The induced current always flows in the direction that opposes the change causing it. If the coil falls toward a magnet, the induced current creates a magnetic field that pushes the coil away — slowing its fall. Nature resists change.

What Happens Step by Step

  1. The coil enters the magnetic field. Flux through the coil increases.
  2. By Faraday's Law, a voltage is induced in the coil.
  3. Current flows through the coil in a direction that creates an opposing field (Lenz's Law).
  4. The opposing field exerts an upward force on the falling coil, braking its descent.
  5. As the coil exits the field, flux decreases, the current reverses, and a braking force appears again.

Real-World Applications

💡
Electric GeneratorsTurbines spin coils inside magnets. Changing flux continuously induces current — producing the AC electricity in your home.
🚂
Magnetic BrakesEddy current brakes on trains use the same principle — induced currents oppose motion and slow the vehicle without physical contact.
📱
Wireless ChargingA changing current in the charger pad creates a changing field, which induces current in the phone's coil to charge its battery.
🔊
MicrophonesSound vibrates a membrane attached to a coil. The coil moves in a magnetic field, inducing a current that mirrors the sound wave.

Practice Problems

Think about the direction of forces and the relationship between flux change and induced effects.

Easy1. A coil falls toward the north pole of a magnet. The flux through the coil is increasing. Does the induced current create a north or south pole facing the magnet (to oppose the approach)?

Hint: By Lenz's Law, the induced field opposes the approach. Like poles repel — so the coil must present a north pole toward the magnet's north pole.

Easy2. If the coil moves faster through the field, the induced current will be…

Hint: Faster motion → faster change in flux → larger induced EMF → larger current.

Medium3. Doubling the number of turns in the coil while keeping everything else the same will…

Hint: EMF = N × (dΦ/dt). Doubling N doubles the EMF.

Challenge4. A coil with 50 turns experiences a flux change of 0.04 Wb in 0.1 s. What is the induced EMF? (EMF = N × ΔΦ/Δt)

Hint: EMF = 50 × 0.04 / 0.1 = 20 V
