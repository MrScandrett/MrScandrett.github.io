Camera Exposure Triangle

Aperture Shutter Speed ISO Depth of Field · Motion · Noise

Every correctly exposed shot balances three controls at once. Push one, and you have to pull another to keep the same brightness — but each one drags a side effect along with it. Feel that three-way trade-off below before you ever touch a real camera.

The Three Corners

🔘
Aperture How wide the lens opens (f-stop). Wider = more light, but a shallower zone in focus.
⏱️
Shutter Speed How long the sensor is exposed. Slower = more light, but motion blurs.
📶
ISO How much the sensor amplifies light. Higher = brighter in the dark, but grainier.

Go Deeper: Exposure Value (EV)

Exposure is really just "how much total light reached the sensor." Aperture, shutter speed, and ISO each add or subtract light in fixed steps called stops. One full stop always doubles or halves the light — which means you can always trade one control for another and land on the exact same exposure, just with different side effects. That's the whole triangle in one sentence: same brightness, different trade-offs.

Practice Problems

Easy1. You need to freeze a fast-moving basketball. Which control matters most?

Hint: A slow shutter is what causes motion blur — speeding it up freezes action.

Easy2. Widening the aperture (a smaller f-number) does what to depth of field?

Hint: Portrait photographers open the aperture wide specifically to blur the background.

Medium3. Raising ISO to shoot in a dark room brightens the image, but at what cost?

Hint: ISO doesn't add real light — it amplifies the signal, and amplifying noise along with it.

Medium4. You open the aperture one full stop (more light). To keep exposure exactly the same, what should you do to shutter speed or ISO?

Hint: If one corner adds a stop of light, another corner has to give a stop back to stay balanced.

Challenge5. A student wants a blurry background AND frozen action in bright daylight. Why is that combination hard, and what's the usual fix?

Hint: Wide aperture + fast shutter both want low light already — in bright sun that combo lets in too much. A neutral-density filter blocks light without changing focus or motion.
