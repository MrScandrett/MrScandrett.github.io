    1 · The Link Is a UHF Radio Wave

    A bodypack converts your mic audio onto an FM or digital carrier frequency inside the 470–608 MHz UHF TV band. Whip antennas perform best near a quarter wavelength — around 15 cm at 500 MHz.

    2 · Why Separate Channels Still Collide (Intermodulation)

    Every RF receiver front-end amplifier has a cubic nonlinearity (y = a₁x + a₂x² + a₃x³). Mixing transmitters on f₁ and f₂ creates phantom third-order frequencies you never dialed:

    2f₁ − f₂  ·  2f₂ − f₁  ·  f₁ + f₂ − f₃

    Spacing transmitters evenly is the worst trap: every carrier’s product lands smack on the next transmitter’s frequency. Look for the same nonlinear math in gain staging and Fourier distortion.

    4 · Line of Sight, Diversity Receivers & Bodypack Hygiene

    Human bodies, steel scenery, and brick walls block UHF radio waves. Test what happens when Performer B steps clear into sightline:

    Diversity & Antenna Distribution

    True diversity receivers compare two spaced antennas and switch instantaneously to avoid multipath dead spots. An antenna distribution box feeds one well-placed pair of antennas to up to 8 receivers cleanly.

    Bodypack Placement & Sweat Protection

    Salt water in stage sweat absorbs UHF RF and corrodes lav capsules. Mount packs inside a neoprene waist pouch or over dry fabric away from bare skin, keep the quarter-wave whip straight (never folded), and tie a loose strain-relief loop in the mic cable.
