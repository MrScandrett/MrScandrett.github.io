Stage plotting

Before moving the scenery, make a plan everyone can read. A ground plan shows the stage from above: where things stand, how much space they need, and where people can move. Design one scene of your musical, test the layout, then print it for your crew.

Use the performer’s directions

Stage left and stage right belong to a performer facing the audience. With the audience at the bottom of this plan, stage left is on the page’s right. Downstage is toward the audience; upstage is away from them.

The dashed centerline splits the stage into left and right. The front edge here is the downstage edge of our simplified playing area.

Measure footprints

Measure your usable playing area and each object’s footprint: the floor space it occupies. A 6-foot bench needs twice the width of a 3-foot table in the same plan.

The grid and scale bar use the units you select. The printed plan fits the page; read the dimensions rather than measuring the paper with a ruler. This tool models a rectangular, end-on stage.

Leave room for the show

Plot scenery and performers, then reserve clear areas for entrances, exits, crossings, and shifts. Test the routes in rehearsal with the real costumes and props.

Record the approved wing storage and quick-change locations in your notes. This plan shows the playing area; wing space, rigging, object heights, and audience sightlines need a separate venue check with your teacher.

Design, rehearse, revise

  1. Measure the usable stage. Enter its width and depth. Name your production, scene, and revision.
  2. Add scenery and equipment. Rename every item; enter its real width, depth, and center position.
  3. Place at least three performers. Add a clear area for an entrance or shift. Rotate items as needed.
  4. Use Check layout, then trace each actor’s route with a partner. Check views from audience seats and agree on clearance with your teacher.
  5. Write rehearsal changes in your notes. Print the plan and item list, then use Cue Sheets to record when items move.
Practice challenge: a market scene

Load the example. Move the lead to downstage left, keep both clear routes open, and give the bench a useful playing position. Explain how your choices support the action. The example dimensions are invented; replace them with your school’s measurements.
