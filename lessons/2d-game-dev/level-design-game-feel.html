Lesson 09 · 70 minutes · Design layer

Teach with the level. Reward with feel.

Great levels teach without text: introduce an idea safely, develop it, then twist it. Game feel — squash and stretch, particles, screen shake, hit-stop and sound — makes every action readable. You will redesign the level and add juice.

01 / WHY

Where this comes from

The first screen is the tutorial.

1985 → 2013

Super Mario Bros.’ opening screen teaches running, jumping and enemies before a single word appears. Decades later, phone games like Flappy Bird and idle games like Cookie Clicker proved that juicy feedback — a pop, a number, a shake — can make even one tap satisfying. The Clicker and Rhythm tutorials are built on that feedback.

Introduce

Show a new idea where failure is cheap: a single spike on flat ground.

Develop

Combine it with something known: spikes before a gap.

Twist

Change one thing: the planks over spikes, or a parcel you must drop down to reach.

Rest

After a hard part, give a checkpoint and a flat run. Tension needs release.

04 / TEST

Playtest protocol

Watch, don’t explain.

  1. Hand over the controls and stay quiet.Your explanations won’t ship with the game.
  2. Write down every death and every pause.Where did they hesitate? Where did they fail twice?
  3. Ask three questions after: What was the goal? What was hardest? What felt best?
  4. Change one thing, test again.Then it’s evidence, not opinion.
