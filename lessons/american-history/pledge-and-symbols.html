The pledge & national symbols

Read closely, explore the history of the pledge, and connect its words to civic responsibilities.

Words to examine together

Read the pledge

I pledge allegiance to the Flag of the United States of America, and to the Republic for which it stands, one Nation under God, indivisible, with liberty and justice for all.

Discuss: What could “liberty and justice for all” look like in our classroom?

The pledge has a history, too

Francis Bellamy wrote an early version in 1892, more than a century after independence. Its wording changed over time; “under God” was added in 1954. The pledge is a civic tradition, while the Constitution is the framework of government. Understanding that difference helps us connect words, rights, and responsibilities.

Read the flag

The flag’s fifty stars represent the states; its thirteen stripes represent the original colonies. Symbols can carry a shared meaning while inviting different personal responses.

Try it: Choose one phrase from the pledge. Explain it in your own words, then give an example of a responsibility it suggests.
