The states & our union

Explore fifty states, distinguish states from the federal district, and investigate a place of your choice.

One country, many places

The United States has fifty states. Forty-eight form the contiguous group; Alaska and Hawaii are also states. Washington, D.C., is the federal district, rather than a state. U.S. territories are distinct from states.

States have their own governments, while the federal government serves the country as a whole. A state capital is the seat of its state government; it need not be the state’s largest city.

Sources: U.S. Geological Survey: geographic definitions · Census Bureau: geographic units

Investigate a state

  1. Choose your home state and a state you have never visited.
  2. Use the official state websites to find each capital and one feature of its geography.
  3. Record one question about the Indigenous peoples and history of each place. Modern state boundaries do not describe the full history of the land.
  4. Compare the two places: what is similar, and what would you like to understand better?

Exit ticket

Name one state, its capital, and one fact you verified. Include the source. Explain why Washington state and Washington, D.C., are different places.
