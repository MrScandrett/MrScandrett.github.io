Planet Field Guide · Stop 6 · Gas Giant

Jupiter: King of Planets & Gravitational Sculptor

Containing more than twice the mass of all other solar system planets combined, Jupiter is a giant sphere of hydrogen and helium spinning once every 9.9 hours. Its gravity carved gaps in the asteroid belt, protects inner planets from some comets, and heats the interiors of its largest moons.

Driving question: How does Jupiter’s gravity keep Io volcanically active and Europa’s subsurface ocean liquid—far outside the Sun’s habitable zone?

The 4 : 2 : 1 Laplace Resonance & Tidal Heating

For every 1 orbit of Ganymede, Europa completes 2 orbits and Io completes 4. These repeating gravitational alignments tug Io and Europa into slightly oval paths, flexing their rocky and icy interiors like kneading clay—powering volcanoes on Io and a buried ocean on Europa.

Atmospheric Dynamics

The Great Red Spot & Giant Magnetosphere

Jupiter's Great Red Spot is an anticyclonic storm wider than Earth that has been spinning for at least 150 years. Decades of telescope and spacecraft images show it slowly shrinking in width while growing taller—fueled by banded jet streams racing in opposite directions.

Spacecraft Exploration

How We Explored Jupiter

  • Voyager 1 & 2 (1979): Discovered active sulfur volcanoes on Io, Jupiter’s faint ring, and lightning in cloud belts.
  • Galileo (1995–2003): Dropped an atmospheric entry probe into Jupiter and revealed strong evidence for Europa’s buried liquid ocean.
  • Juno (2016–present) & Europa Clipper (launched 2024): Juno’s polar orbits mapped Jupiter’s fuzzy core and polar cyclones; Europa Clipper is en route to test Europa’s ocean habitability.
