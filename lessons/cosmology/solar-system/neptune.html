Planet Field Guide · Stop 9 · Ice Giant

Neptune: The Supersonic Wind World

Orbiting 30.1 times farther from the Sun than Earth, Neptune receives less than 0.11% of Earth’s sunlight—yet hosts the fastest winds in the solar system, screaming at up to 2,100 km/h. Predicted by math before it was ever seen through a telescope, Neptune anchors the inner edge of the Kuiper Belt.

Driving question: How can the farthest major planet power faster winds than Jupiter or Venus while receiving a thousand times less sunlight?

Discovery & Dynamics

Notable Features

Found by Pure Math (1846)

Tiny gravitational tugs in Uranus’s orbit allowed Urbain Le Verrier and John Couch Adams to calculate Neptune’s position before Johann Galle spotted it.

Triton’s Nitrogen Geysers

Voyager 2 photographed dark nitrogen-ice geysers erupting 8 km high on Triton’s −235 °C cantaloupe-textured crust.

Dark Spots & Methane Cirrus

Transient Earth-sized anticyclonic storms appear and vanish over years, topped by bright white clouds of frozen methane crystal.

Spacecraft Exploration

How We Explored Neptune

  • Voyager 2 Flyby (August 1989): The only spacecraft to visit Neptune, swooping within 4,950 km of its north polar clouds before diving past Triton, revealing active geysers, faint ring arcs, and supersonic storms.
  • Hubble & JWST Deep Monitoring: Tracked new dark vortices, vanishing cloud bands, and crisp infrared views of Neptune’s faint rings and moons.
