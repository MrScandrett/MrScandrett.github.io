Imagine it · Make it · Share it

Big ideas start on a flat surface.

A line can become a character. A few colors can set a mood. A picture can tell a whole story before anyone reads a word.

That is the magic of 2D design: using shapes, colors, images, and words to make people see and feel something. From the posters in your hallway to your favorite game characters, design is everywhere—and you can make it too. You don't have to be an expert artist to begin. Curiosity is enough.

Your first creative challenge

On paper or in a drawing tool, use only a circle, a triangle, and a rectangle to create a character. Change their sizes, colors, and positions. Can you make one version look friendly and another look mysterious? Show someone and ask what they notice first.

Think like a designer: Which choice changed the feeling most? There is no single right answer—try an idea, look closely, and try again.

Interactive Warm-Up · How Flat Screens Fake Depth

Can a flat picture feel deep?

Artists can make a flat screen feel like a world you could step into. Turn the buttons off and on, then move the slider. Which change makes the biggest difference? These visual clues help artists, animators, and game designers create a sense of distance.

All 5 cues active: foreground shapes overlap midground, shrink toward the horizon, fade into sky blue, and scroll at different parallax speeds.
