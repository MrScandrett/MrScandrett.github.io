Draw It · Foundation lesson · Grades 4–12 · 50–60 minutes

Drawing Foundations: Shape, Line & Value

Draw a real object before inventing its polished version. You will make an observation sheet with a shape map, a contour drawing, and a shaded drawing using five values.

Prepare your subject

Choose a cup, shoe, leaf, toy, or another object you can keep in front of you. Put it under one steady lamp. Use a pencil, eraser, paper, and a dark pen or pencil. Look at the object more often than the page. Do not trace a photograph.

Look for: the largest simple shapes, their height-to-width ratio, the empty spaces between parts (negative space), the edge where the object meets its background (contour), and the direction of the light.

1. Map shapes and proportions · 10 minutes

  1. Draw a light box around the whole object. Compare its height with its width. Is it twice as tall, nearly square, or wider than tall?
  2. Block in two to four simple shapes. A mug might begin as an oval, a cylinder, and the open shape inside its handle.
  3. Check the negative space. Measure the handle opening or the gap between parts against the object. Adjust the shapes before drawing details.

Shape map: the handle opening is a shape you can measure, even though it is empty.

Contour: follow the observed edges. Change line weight where an edge falls into shadow.

2. Draw the contour · 10 minutes

On a second part of the page, redraw the outside edge slowly. Then add only the important inside edges. Pause at each change of direction. Compare your drawing with the object: where does the rim begin, and where does the handle attach? Correct one proportion before adding texture.

Quick check: hide the object. Could a classmate recognize it from the outline alone? If not, simplify the large shape and check the negative spaces again.

3. Give the shape form with five values · 15 minutes

Make a scale from paper white (1) to your darkest pencil mark (5). Use one light direction. Leave the lightest area almost white, shade the planes that turn away from the light, and place the darkest value in the deepest shadow or narrow contact point. The cast shadow on the table should point away from the lamp.

12345
  1. Mark the lamp direction with an arrow outside your drawing.
  2. Circle the brightest spot and locate the darkest shadow on the real object.
  3. Assign each large region one of the five values before blending or adding texture.
  4. Squint at both object and drawing. The big light and dark masses should match.

Common repair: if everything looks equally gray, preserve more white and deepen one true shadow. If the form looks flat, make the value change follow the surface turning away from the light.

4. Invent a version · 10 minutes

Draw the object once more from memory as a poster emblem, game prop, or character accessory. Keep its most recognizable contour. Change one feature to communicate a purpose or mood, and use three of your five values to keep the form readable at a small size. Compare this version with your observed drawing and label what you kept and what you changed.

Continue digitally in the Vector Drawing Studio for clean editable shapes, or use the Layer Lab for textured raster artwork.

Critique and finish

Show a partner the object and your three drawings. Ask: “Which proportion looks most accurate? Where does the light come from? Can you recognize the invented version without its label?” Revise one specific line or value after their answer.

Finish when: your sheet includes a measured shape map, a recognizable contour, a five-step scale, a drawing with a consistent light direction, an invented variation, and one documented revision.

For younger artists: use a simple object and three values (white, gray, dark), then expand to five. Extend: draw the same object from a second viewpoint and compare how its negative spaces change.
