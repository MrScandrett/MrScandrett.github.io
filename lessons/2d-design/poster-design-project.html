Brief → sketch → critique → revise. Combine vector art, color, layout, and typography into a poster for a real audience.

1. Vector Drawing Studio 2. Composition & Layout Studio 3. Poster Design Project

The brief: invite your community

Design a poster for a STEAM showcase. Your audience is families who may know nothing about the event. They must quickly find what, when, where, and what to do next. Use a real approved event or clearly label your work as a practice event. Do not publish personal contact details.

A complete design process

  1. Plan (10 minutes). Write your audience and message. Draw three small thumbnail sketches with different focal points. Pick one and explain why.
  2. Build (20 minutes). Use the Poster Design Studio above—or import an SVG mark you built in Vector Drawing Studio. Choose a template, limited palette, high-contrast ink, and clear hierarchy.
  3. Critique (10 minutes). Open the Critique tab and run the 5-Second Audience Test with a partner. Ask them to recall the event and find its date and location. Record their actual answers below.
  4. Revise (10 minutes). Save a Before snapshot, then change one choice based on the feedback. Compare before and after, and explain how the revision helps your audience.
  5. Deliver (5 minutes). Export SVG for editable vector artwork or PNG for a screen preview. For printing, use Print / PDF and check the paper size, margins, and a physical proof. These exports do not add printer bleed or a CMYK profile; ask your printer about those requirements.

Review your finished work

  • Message: a viewer can find all four required details without your explanation.
  • Craft: consistent alignment, intentional spacing, ≥ 4.5:1 text contrast, and a clear emblem silhouette.
  • Iteration: three sketches, peer feedback, and a justified before/after revision.
  • Delivery: the exported file opens correctly; original work or sources and licenses are credited.

Critique & revision notes

Finish when: Submit your brief, three sketches, before/after designs, peer feedback, credits, and a final export. Explain one design decision that helped a viewer.

For younger designers: work with a partner, sketch with paper shapes, and explain your choices aloud. Extend: duplicate your poster JSON and redesign it for a second audience, then compare the two.
