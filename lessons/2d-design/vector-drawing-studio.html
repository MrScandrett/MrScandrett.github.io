Select: Shift-click adds to the selection; drag on empty space to box-select. Drag the square handles to resize (Shift keeps proportions, Alt from the centre) and the round handle to rotate (Shift snaps to 15°). Alt-drag an object to copy it. Double-click a grouped object to pick it alone; double-click a shape to edit its points.

Pen: click for a corner, drag for a curve, Alt while dragging for a sharp turn. Click the first point to close; Enter or Escape finishes an open path.

Points: drag points or handles; Shift-click or box-select several. Alt-drag a handle to break a smooth pair. Double-click a point to switch corner ↔ smooth; double-click the outline to add a point. Delete removes points.

Arrow keys nudge (Shift = 10). Ctrl/⌘: Z undo, Shift Z redo, A select all, C copy, V paste, D duplicate, G group, Shift G ungroup, ] forward, [ backward. Shortcuts work while the studio is focused.

Five moves to your own mark

01 / BUILD

Start with geometry

Sketch three ideas on paper. Choose New, then combine at least three objects: a circle, a rectangle, a polygon or star, or a pen shape. Use two intentional colors.

○ Add three shapes

02 / BEND

Give a curve character

With the Pen, press and drag to pull out a curve, or double-click a shape and drag one of its round handles. Predict which part will bend; notice that the square points stay put unless you move them.

○ Edit a curve handle

03 / COMBINE

Build new shapes from old

Overlap two shapes, select both, and try Unite, Minus, Intersect and Exclude. A crescent is one circle minus another; a keyhole is a circle united with a triangle.

○ Combine two shapes

04 / ARRANGE

Make overlap work

Move one object in front of another using the Objects panel. Hide it to inspect what is underneath. Group the parts of your mark, then use Align to centre it and compare the balance.

○ Change the stacking order

05 / REFINE

Make it travel

Squint at your mark. Would it still be recognizable as a tiny icon? Remove a detail, ask a partner what they see, then export an SVG and save the editable project.

○ Export your SVG

These checks notice actions, not design quality. Use the brief and partner feedback to decide whether your emblem works.

Know what you’re moving

The ideas behind the tools

Fill & stroke. Fill colors a shape’s inside. Stroke traces its edge. An open curve can still have a fill: the renderer connects its endpoints to close the filled area.

Anchors & handles. Square anchors are points the path passes through; round handles pull the tangent direction and bend of the cubic Bézier curve on each side. A smooth point keeps its two handles in a straight line, so the curve flows through it; a corner point lets them turn independently. Handles guide the path without sitting on it.

Objects & layers. Each shape is independent. The stack controls what covers what. Overlapping same-colored shapes can look like one silhouette, but they remain separate editable objects here.

Boolean operations. Unite combines areas, Minus front cuts the front shapes out of the back one, Intersect keeps only their overlap, and Exclude keeps everything except the overlap. The result is one new path with its own editable points, the same idea as Illustrator’s Pathfinder or Inkscape’s Path menu. Overlapping shapes are still a good way to sketch: combine them once the silhouette works.

Vector & raster. SVG keeps drawing instructions and editable shapes. PNG turns the result into pixels. Choose SVG for a scalable logo and PNG when an app needs an ordinary image. Text in SVG uses the receiving computer’s available fonts.

Critique & revision

Explain the choices, not just the tools.

Finish when: you have an original emblem, a deliberately edited curve, an SVG, an editable project, and a reason for your choices. For a younger designer, build with a partner and explain aloud. Extend by adapting your emblem for a second audience.
