From pixels to paths

A raster image stores a grid of colored pixels. A vector drawing stores instructions for shapes and paths, so its edges stay sharp when enlarged. Use raster for photographs; use vectors for icons, signs and logos.

A fill colors the inside of a closed shape; a stroke traces its edge. A path joins anchor points. A cubic Bézier curve uses two control points to pull the direction and bend between its endpoints.

Try → explain → make

  1. Move the curve handle below. Predict which part of the leaf changes before moving it. The endpoints stay fixed.
  2. Compare a filled silhouette with an outlined emblem. Does it still read at the smallest size?
  3. Use the overlap setting to compare two separate shapes with a merged silhouette. In a full vector editor, union combines areas, difference cuts one away, and intersection keeps only their overlap.
  4. Sketch three different emblems on paper. Build your chosen idea in any vector editor using circles, rectangles and one edited path; use this lab to investigate its curves and style.

Critique & revision notes

Finish when: Your emblem reads as a silhouette at 32 pixels, uses a deliberately edited curve, and exports as an SVG. Explain when you would choose raster instead.

For younger designers: work with a partner, sketch with paper shapes, and explain your choices aloud. Extend: make a second version for a different audience and compare the results.
