Give the eye a route

Hierarchy establishes what is seen first, second and third. Size, contrast and position all help. Alignment gives separate elements a shared edge. Proximity groups related information; leave more space between groups than inside them.

Negative space is the unoccupied area around your content. It gives the eye room to separate ideas. Balance can be symmetrical or asymmetrical: a small dark shape can counter a larger light one.

Three experiments

  1. Switch between shared-edge and staggered alignment. Which layout makes the details easier to find? Explain rather than assuming one style always wins.
  2. Change the margin, headline size and space between details one at a time. Notice when a large title wraps or too much space makes the event details feel unrelated.
  3. Hide the guides, show a partner the layout for five seconds, then ask what they saw first and what the event is. Revise one variable and repeat.

Critique & revision notes

Saving a project also keeps these notes. SVG and PNG contain the poster artwork.

Finish when: Export two contrasting layouts. Record what your partner noticed first, then explain one revision using alignment, proximity or negative space.

For younger designers: work with a partner, sketch with paper shapes, and explain your choices aloud. Extend: make a second version for a different audience and compare the results.
