Core Physics

Why Momentum Never Changes in an Isolated System

René Descartes and Isaac Newton called momentum the "quantity of motion." Unlike kinetic energy (which only has magnitude and easily converts into heat), momentum is a vector pointing in the direction of velocity—and it cannot disappear unless an outside push acts on the system.

Linear Momentum (click symbols to explore)
p = mv

Mass (kg) times velocity (m/s). A heavy slow freight train carries more momentum than a tiny fast bullet because both mass and velocity enter linearly.

Newton's Second Law (General Form)
F_net = Δp ÷ Δt

Newton originally wrote his Second Law in terms of momentum, not F = ma. That makes it work even when mass changes, such as a rocket burning fuel.

Conservation Balance
m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = m₁v₁' + m₂v₂'

When external net force is zero, the vector sum of all individual momenta before an interaction equals the vector sum afterward.

Kinetic Energy (click symbols to compare)
KE = ½mv²

Because speed is squared, kinetic energy cares far more about speed than mass—and in inelastic crashes, part of KE turns into heat, sound, or bending metal.

Newton's 3rd Law → Conservation Proof

During a collision between two carts, Cart 1 pushes Cart 2 with force F₁→₂, and by Newton's Third Law, Cart 2 pushes back on Cart 1 with equal and opposite force F₂→₁ = −F₁→₂.

Both carts stay in contact for the exact same duration Δt. Multiplying both sides by Δt gives equal and opposite impulses: Δp₁ = −Δp₂, so Δp₁ + Δp₂ = 0. Meanwhile, the system's center of mass keeps moving at a constant velocity v_COM.

Deeper Reason — Noether's Theorem (1918):

Why does nature conserve momentum even in quantum fields and curved spacetime where "push and pull" forces aren't even defined? Mathematician Emmy Noether proved that every conservation law comes from a symmetry of nature. Because the laws of physics work the same everywhere in space (spatial translation symmetry—moving an experiment 5 meters sideways changes nothing), linear momentum must be conserved.

When can we treat a real crash as isolated? A car crash or bat-ball impact lasts only milliseconds. During that brief instant, internal impact forces reach thousands of newtons while external gravity or road friction delivers negligible impulse—making conservation accurate to better than 99%.

Airbags & Crumple Zones

Whether you stop against concrete or an airbag, Δp = −mv is identical. Doubling stopping time cuts force in half; stretching a 12 ms crash to 240 ms slashes peak force by 20×.

Sports Follow-Through

When hitting a baseball or golf ball, the goal is maximum Δp rather than safety. Swinging through the ball lengthens contact Δt at high force, maximizing the area under F(t).

Why Bouncing Hurts More

Stopping from +v to 0 changes momentum by mv. Rebounding at −v changes velocity from +v to −v, delivering Δp = 2mv—twice the impulse of sticking!

Modern Physics

Near Light Speed: Why Momentum Climbs Toward Infinity

Classical p = mv breaks down near the speed of light c. An object's rest mass m never changes, yet Einstein's relativity multiplies momentum by the Lorentz factor γ (gamma). As speed approaches c, γ shoots upward without limit—meaning finite impulses yield smaller and smaller speed gains, and nothing with mass can ever reach c.

Lorentz Factor (click to explore)
γ = 1/√(1−v²/c²)

At everyday speeds γ ≈ 1.000000. At 0.866 c, γ = 2.00; in CERN's Large Hadron Collider, protons reach γ ≈ 7,454.

Rest Energy (click to explore)
E = mc²

Even at zero momentum (p = 0), mass itself stores energy equal to mc², the base leg of the energy–momentum triangle.

Full Energy–Momentum Relation
E² = (pc)² + (mc²)²

Unifies massive particles and massless light: for a photon (m = 0), it simplifies directly to E = pc.

Sunlight bouncing off a giant reflective sail delivers twice the impulse of light absorbed on a black surface—propelling fuel-free spacecraft like JAXA's IKAROS and LightSail 2 across the solar system.

Conceptual Depth & History

Classic Momentum Puzzles & Discovery Timeline

Puzzle 1: You are stranded floating motionless 10 meters from your space station holding a heavy wrench. How do you get back?

Throw the wrench directly away from the station! Initially total momentum is zero. When you push the wrench outward with momentum +p, it exerts an equal backward impulse giving you −p toward the hatch. In frictionless space, even a gentle throw delivers steady return drift.

Puzzle 2: Which hits a window harder—a hailstone that bounces off elastically or an identical-mass snowball that splats and sticks?

The bouncing hailstone delivers up to twice the impulse! Stopping a snowball from +v to 0 changes momentum by mv. Reversing a hailstone from +v to −v changes momentum by 2mv, transferring double the average force over the same contact time.

Puzzle 3: How can a rocket accelerate in the vacuum of space when there is no air to push against?

It pushes against its own fuel! Inside the combustion chamber, expanding gas pushes forward on the forward wall while escaping out the nozzle—creating an action–reaction pair between rocket and exhaust. Air outside actually gets in the way and slightly reduces thrust.

Puzzle 4: Why doesn't lifting 1 ball on Newton's cradle ever launch 2 balls at half speed?

Momentum would allow it, but kinetic energy forbids it! Two balls at speed v/2 have momentum 2m(v/2) = mv, yet kinetic energy 2 × ½m(v/2)² = ¼mv²—only half the initial ½mv². Hardened steel spheres cannot destroy half the energy on elastic impact.

Timeline: From "Quantity of Motion" to Cosmic Symmetry

  1. 1644 · René Descartes: In Principles of Philosophy, Descartes argues that God preserves a fixed total "quantity of motion" (m|v|), though he omits velocity direction.
  2. 1668 · Royal Society Collision Proofs: John Wallis, Christopher Wren, and Christiaan Huygens independently prove that signed vector momentum (mv) is conserved across elastic and inelastic impacts.
  3. 1687 · Isaac Newton: Principia Mathematica states Force as rate of change of momentum and pairs action–reaction forces, making conservation a direct theorem.
  4. 1903 · Konstantin Tsiolkovsky: Derives Δv = v_e ln(m₀/m_f) by integrating continuous exhaust momentum in vacuum, launching astronautics theory.
  5. 1905 · Albert Einstein: Special relativity introduces the Lorentz factor γ into p = γmv and reveals that massless light quanta carry momentum p = E/c.
  6. 1918 · Emmy Noether: Proves Noether's theorem—establishing that conservation of linear momentum follows rigorously from spatial translation symmetry of physical law.

Review & Apply

Key Vocabulary & Practice Problems

Momentum (p = mv)
Vector quantity equal to mass times velocity (kg·m/s), pointing in the exact direction of motion.
Isolated System
A collection of interacting objects on which the net external force (and external impulse) is zero.
Impulse (J = F_avg Δt = Δp)
Force integrated over contact time; equals the total change in an object's momentum.
Coefficient of Restitution (e)
Ratio of relative separation speed to relative approach speed: 1 for perfectly elastic bounce, 0 when objects stick.
Center of Mass (COM)
The mass-weighted average position of a system; moves at constant velocity whenever external forces vanish.
Noether's Theorem
Emmy Noether's proof that spatial translation symmetry guarantees conservation of linear momentum.

Solved: 0 / 7

Easy1. Why does an airbag reduce injury force compared with hitting a rigid steering wheel during the same crash?

Hint: Your head starts at speed v and stops at 0 either way, so Δp is fixed. Since F = Δp / Δt, making Δt larger shrinks F.

Easy2. A 70 kg sprinter runs at 8.0 m/s. What is the magnitude of the sprinter's momentum in kg·m/s?

Hint: p = m × v = 70 kg × 8.0 m/s = 560 kg·m/s.

Medium3. A 3.0 kg cart sliding at +4.0 m/s collides and sticks to a stationary 1.0 kg cart. What is their shared final speed in m/s?

Hint: p_before = 3.0 × 4.0 + 0 = 12.0 kg·m/s. Combined mass is 4.0 kg, so v_f = 12.0 ÷ 4.0 = 3.0 m/s.

Medium4. What average net force (in Newtons) stops a 2.0 kg ball moving at 15 m/s in 0.050 s?

Hint: Impulse magnitude is Δp = 2.0 × 15 = 30 N·s. Then F_avg = 30 ÷ 0.050 = 600 N.

Challenge5. A motionless 60 kg astronaut throws a 2.0 kg wrench forward at 9.0 m/s. What is the astronaut's recoil speed in m/s?

Hint: Equal and opposite momenta: 60 × v_astro = 2.0 × 9.0 = 18, so v_astro = 18 ÷ 60 = 0.30 m/s.

Challenge6. A 0.50 kg rubber ball hits a brick wall at +10.0 m/s and rebounds straight backward at −10.0 m/s. What is the magnitude of the impulse delivered to the ball in N·s?

Hint: Δv = v_f − v_i = −10.0 − (+10.0) = −20.0 m/s, so |J| = 0.50 × 20.0 = 10.0 N·s!

Challenge7. A 4.0 kg cart moving at 6.0 m/s strikes and locks onto an identical 4.0 kg cart initially at rest. How many Joules of kinetic energy are converted into heat and sound?

Hint: KE_before = ½(4)(6²) = 72 J. Final shared speed is 3.0 m/s (8 kg total), so KE_after = ½(8)(3²) = 36 J. Lost = 72 − 36 = 36 J.
