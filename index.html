Recent Updates

  1. Updated Cymatics: a real Chladni plate 2026-10-29

    The plate simulator now solves the real plate equation: choose steel, aluminium, brass, glass or acrylic, set the size and thickness, drive it with a wave driver or a violin bow, and watch sand hop only where the plate beats gravity. Repeat Faraday's vacuum experiment with fine powder, read the real frequency, displacement and g-force, and work through seven experiments.

  2. Updated Drum Lab: a Funky Rhythm Ladder you can print 2026-10-29

    The funk ladder is now a method-book page: your hands keep one pattern while each rung changes one thing (bass drum on eighths, then sixteenths, ghost notes, open hi-hat, sixteenth hi-hat), numbered 1 to 68, with a final rung built only from parts you have played. Each exercise plays four times after a count-in, and every rung prints as a one-page handout with tempo boxes, with or without Save ink.

  3. Updated Egyptian Hieroglyphs: from royal cartouches to the Rosetta Stone 2026-10-29

    The six-sign starter is now a full scribe's workshop: type with all 24 one-consonant signs, flip inscriptions to see why you read into the faces, open the cartouches of Tutankhamun, Hatshepsut, Ramesses II, Ptolemy V and Cleopatra, spell your own name in a cartouche you can save, repeat Champollion's Ptolemaios–Kleopatra comparison, solve determinative cases, and fill a grain measure with Eye of Horus fractions (then check whether that famous story holds up). Museum photos of the Rosetta Stone, the Stela of Minnakht, the Papyrus of Ani and Tutankhamun's pectoral open in the lightbox.

  4. Updated Ezekiel: the whole story, and where it leads 2026-10-29

    Read Ezekiel as a story in three acts, from the throne-chariot by the canal to the river that heals the Dead Sea, then follow eight threads from Ezekiel to Jesus, the Spirit and Revelation's new creation.

  5. Updated Revelation, retold in nine acts 2026-10-29

    Revelation is now a full illuminated lesson: the whole story retold act by act from Patmos to the New Jerusalem, the seven cities and their history, a Roman coin with seven stars, the papyrus that reads 616, an echo finder for its Old Testament images, the sevens explained, and four ways Christians have read it.

  6. Updated Seeing Light Lab, rebuilt with real science 2026-10-29

    Follow light from a lamp to a ball to your eye, watch a pupil change size at its real width from starlight to sunshine, see the upside-down picture on your retina, and watch colors fade to gray as your rods take over in the dark. Plus hands-on tests and public-domain photos from NASA, Gray's Anatomy and Ibn al-Haytham's Book of Optics.

  7. New lesson Anatomy of a Computer 2026-10-28

    Computer Science · Explore computer hardware, compare RAM and storage, connect applications to operating systems, and trace a website request across the internet.

  8. New lesson Arnaut Daniel: The Sestina Workshop 2026-10-28

    Language & Literature · Trace the six recurring end words of a sestina and use the pattern to draft a stanza.

  9. New lesson Chord Progressions & Arranging 2026-10-28

    Music · Translate registered Fake Book songs into Roman numerals and Nashville numbers, transpose their changes, and create a rehearsal chart and ensemble arrangement.

  10. New lesson Drop Voicings: Open Up the Chord 2026-10-28

    Music · Stack a seventh chord in close position, count voices from the top, and drop one an octave: Drop 2, Drop 3 and Drop 2 & 4 on a staff, a keyboard and a guitar fretboard, then use them for smooth voice leading through a ii–V–I.

  11. New lesson Edgar Allan Poe: Sound, Suspense & the Uncertain Narrator 2026-10-28

    Language & Literature · Read The Raven aloud with phrase-anchored notes, then trace observation and inference in Poe’s suspense and detective stories.

  12. New lesson Florida: Land, Water & People 2026-10-28

    Complex Systems & Humanities · Begin the 50 States collection with Florida: explore its history, cultures, ecosystems, notable people, and famous events through habitat investigations and an evidence-based field notebook.
