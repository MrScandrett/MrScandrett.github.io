Physics

Forces, motion, waves, optics, and electricity — interactive simulations of the laws that govern the physical world

19 lessons ▾
Lesson 01 · Relativity The Speed of Light 299,792,458 m/s — the universe's speed limit. Explore light-travel times, Einstein's relativity, and why nothing can go faster. Lesson 02 · Electricity and Magnetism Faraday Cage See why a hollow conductor shields its interior, why elevator cars and cars can block signals, and where this idea appears in real life. Lesson 03 · Electricity · Circuits Breadboard Basics No solder required. Learn how solderless breadboards work, explore internal connection strips, and build a working LED circuit step by step. Lesson 04 · Forces & Motion Newton's Laws of Motion Explore all three laws with interactive simulations — inertia, F=ma, and action-reaction. Designed for K–12. Lesson 05 · Fluids & Buoyancy Archimedes' Principle Discover why ships float and rocks sink. Explore density, buoyancy, and the Sink or Float Machine interactive lab. Lesson 06 · Optics & Light Newton's Dispersive Prism In 1666 Newton proved white light is a mixture of all visible colours. Explore dispersion, Snell's Law, and the experimentum crucis with an interactive prism simulator. Lesson 07 · Gravity & Orbits Universal Gravitation Every mass in the universe pulls on every other. Discover why apples fall and why the Moon stays in orbit — with Newton's one equation. Lesson 08 · Electrostatics Coulomb's Law The electric version of gravity — but with two directions. Opposite charges attract, like charges repel, and the force follows an inverse-square law. Lesson 09 · Electromagnetism The Falling Coil Drop a coil through a magnetic field and watch motion become electricity. The current it creates pushes back — Lenz's law in action. Lesson 10 · Induction Faraday's Law Change the magnetic field through a loop and voltage appears. The foundation of every generator, transformer, and electric motor on Earth. Lesson 11 · Wave Mechanics Huygens' Principle Every point on a wavefront is a new source of waves. This single idea explains how light bends around corners, reflects off surfaces, and passes through gaps. Lesson 12 · Wave Mechanics Point Wave A wave from a single source spreads outward in rings — like dropping a pebble in a pond. See how amplitude, frequency, and distance interact. Lesson 13 · Periodic Motion The Pendulum A weight swings back and forth while gravity and momentum trade energy between potential and kinetic. The clock mechanism that kept time for centuries. Lesson 14 · Electrostatics Van de Graaff Balloon Charge builds on a dome, and a lightweight balloon leaps toward or away from it — no contact required. A classic demo of electrostatic force at human scale. Lesson 15 · Electrostatics Van de Graaff Generator A moving belt carries charge to a metal dome until sparks fly. The machine that makes hair stand up and stores hundreds of thousands of volts. Lesson 16 · Optics Optics — Mirrors, Lenses & Light How light reflects off mirrors, bends through lenses, and forms images. The physics behind cameras, telescopes, glasses, and the human eye. Lesson 17 · Kinematics 3D Motion Track position, velocity, and acceleration in three dimensions. The geometry that describes everything from a thrown ball to a spacecraft trajectory. Lesson 18 · Electromagnetism Cathode Ray Tube Fire electrons through a vacuum and steer them with electric and magnetic fields. The technology inside every oscilloscope and television before flat screens. Lesson 19 · Fluid Mechanics Viscosimeter Drop a sphere through a fluid and time its fall. The resistance it meets reveals how thick the fluid is — from water to honey to motor oil.
